SuperInfer

Turning KV Offloading from a Memory Workaround into an SLO Scheduling Primitive

arXiv:2601.20309v2 2026-10-02 RotaSched · VLT/LVF · DuplexKV

DuplexKV reduces16GB bidirectional KV migration from 1556.15ms to 46.80ms. Under high GH200 load, SuperInfer improves TTFT SLO attainment by up to 74.7 pp, but cross-hardware evidence, Pie comparisons, and statistical intervals remain missing.

Core positioning: on tightly coupled CPU–GPU Superchips such as GH200, SuperInfer turns HBM–DRAM KV migration into proactive rotation driven by latency debt. Its contribution integrates scheduling objectives, layout, and full-duplex transfers along the serving critical path, beyond simply adding host memory.

2. Motivation: why900GB/s does not automatically mean fast serving

Online inference couples changing resources: request KV grows linearly with generated length while HBM is fixed. At high arrival rates, new requests wait while old ones occupy HBM, preventing unlimited batch growth. TTFT measures time to first token; TBT measures continuity between tokens. Pauses affect them asymmetrically.

Static strategies reveal the conflict(§3.1, Figure 1, official PDF pp.3–4): Waiting-First improves TTFT but pauses active generations and worsens TBT. Swapped-First protects TBT by restoring offloaded requests, approaching FCFS and underusing swap space. Thus,choosing whom to swap is an SLO decisionrather than passive cleanup after OOM.

Bandwidth is the second problem. PCIe Gen5×16 offers roughly 32–64GB/s one way. Figure 2 shows P99 TTFT/TBT continue improving only as simulated swap bandwidth exceeds PCIe limits(§3.2, PDF p.4). Slow D2H blocks clearing waiting backlogs; slow H2D blocks rotary/swapped requests awaiting restoration.

Most counterintuitively, GH200 NVLink-C2C advertises900GB/s aggregate bidirectional bandwidth, but directly ported vLLM small-block copies reach only about 10 GB/s, below 5%of achievable bandwidth(Figures 4–5, PDF p.4). Grace DRAM is the practical limit: concurrent D2H/H2D reaches approximately 200+200 GB/srather than 450+450GB/s because single-NUMA DRAM bandwidth is 384 GB/s with an overall half-duplex constraint. Fragmented PagedAttention layouts, tiny copies, serialized directions, and races prevent exploiting the fast link.

The quantitative chain is 900GB/s advertised→about 384GB/s measured total DRAM budget→about 10GB/s existing software. SuperInfer aims to approach the realizable concurrent192+192GB/s bound and expose that capacity to SLO scheduling, not exceed physical limits.

3. Argument, assumptions, and trade-offs

The design has two halves.RotaSched Determines who runs, extending waiting/running states with rotary: paused requests whose KV resides in CPU DRAM awaiting rotation.DuplexKV Makes rotation fast through eager block rotation, block-first layouts, batched transfer kernels, and inter-iteration pipelining behind model execution(Figures 6,15, PDF pp.5,9).

Four assumptions underlie the design:

  1. GH200-like Superchips provide much faster CPU–GPU links than PCIe and ample host memory. On one GH200 the paper allocates 400 GB Grace DRAM to KV, reserving 80 GB for OS/runtime(§5.2, PDF p.9).
  2. Model execution can hide scheduling/transfers. Mean main-experiment latency is scheduling 7.63 ms, KV transfer 15.8 ms, and model execution 69.82 ms; only 0.021% of engine iterations stall from incomplete overlap(§5.3.4, PDF pp.11–12).
  3. Fixed TTFT/TBT SLOs express the serving objective. Main TTFT is 5 s、TBT 100 msrather than varying by user, request type, or price tier(§5.2, PDF p.9).
  4. KV migration is lossless: no token pruning or precision reduction, preserving output quality while paying full-byte movement and GH200-specific implementation costs.

Proactive preemption clears waiting backlogs but may harm active-request TBT; larger transfer budgets reduce both tails but need stronger engines; protecting rotary requests sacrifices newcomers. The design exposes positions on the Pareto frontier through interpretable parameters rather than eliminating tradeoffs.

4. Equations and pseudocode

4.1 Virtual Lag Time

RotaSched uses Virtual Lag Time(VLT) to place three states on one priority axis. From§4.2.2 and its equation(PDF p.6):

[ _i = {αReLU(tnow−tlast−βBSB),i∈rotaryReLU(tnow−tarr−βFSF),i∈waiting−(tnow−trun),i∈running\begin{cases} \alpha\,\mathrm{ReLU}(t_{now}-t_{last}-\beta_B S_B), & i\in rotary\\ \mathrm{ReLU}(t_{now}-t_{arr}-\beta_F S_F), & i\in waiting\\ -(t_{now}-t_{run}), & i\in running \end{cases}

]

(S_B) is the TBT SLO and(S_F) the TTFT SLO; t_last is the last-token time, t_arr arrival, and t_run entry into the current running period. Positive VLT means lagging; negative means relatively well served.() controls TBT sensitivity versus TTFT, with(_B,_F) tolerance windows.

4.2 Largest-VLT-First

Each engine iteration:
  If HBM fits every request: fall back to FCFS
  Otherwise:
    1. Compute VLT for waiting/rotary/running requests
    2. Sort into L by descending VLT
    3. Select waiting/rotary requests from L’s head within B_HBM+B_xfer blocks
    4. If space is insufficient, preempt lowest-VLT running requests from the tail
    5. DuplexKV concurrently swaps in/eagerly swaps out; next iteration uses the prepared batch

From§4.2.3 and Figure 10(PDF p.6). B_xfer is the KV-block rotation budget per iteration, converting bandwidth into how much SLO debt can be repaid. The reusable principle is:system capacity should enter scheduling constraints rather than remain hidden in downstream backpressure.

4.3 Avoiding races in DuplexKV

Concurrent H2D restoration and D2H writeback of updated KV can race. DuplexKV eagerly copies full, immutable blocks to DRAM and marks them synced, rather than waiting for preemption. Synced HBM blocks can then be discarded; only unsynced tails require handling. Block-first layouts merge small cross-layer/head segments into roughly 4 MB transfer units, batching same-direction copies into large kernels(§4.3, Figures 11–14, PDF pp.7–8).

6. Datasets, models, metrics, and design

Experiments use NVIDIA GH200 NVL2; each Superchip has 144 GB HBM + 480 GB DRAM, with numactl pinning memory to the same NUMA node(§5.1, PDF p.8). Dense/MoE models are LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen2.5-32B, and Mixtral-8x7B. ShareGPT/LMSYS-Chat-1M requests arrive with Poisson intervals, modeling random arrivals but not production bursts, temporal correlations, or tenant priorities.

SLO attainment is the request fraction meeting TTFT/TBT thresholds. It captures tail experience better than means, but treats5.01s and 50s equally as failures and hides within-threshold distributions. Sensitivity plots therefore also show P99 TTFT/TBT, with throughput separately reported.

Six baselines: vLLM0.6.6.post1(V1 engine), TensorRT-LLM1.1.0, LightLLM Past-Future scheduler, LTR, NEO, and SuperInfer. All enable PagedAttention/chunked prefill. Pie, HeteGen, and Select-N lack public code at the time; FlexGen lacks those features; lossy methods such as InfiniGen are excluded(§5.2, PDF p.9). This keeps implementations runnable but omits the closest GH200 competitor Pie.

8. Layered ablations, hyperparameter sensitivity, and transitions

8.1 Component ablations

Figure 17(PDF p.10) compares vLLM, RotaSched with low budget L(B_xfer=300), RotaSched forced to high budget H(B_xfer=2400), and full SuperInfer. Three conclusions follow:

  1. RotaSched(L) improves TTFT attainment, establishing scheduling value;
  2. forcing2400 blocks through an inefficient engine harms TBT because compute cannot hide transfers;

10. Technical lineage: problems, gaps, and responses

10.1 PagedAttention/vLLM: from fragmentation to block KV

PagedAttention adapts OS virtual memory to KV, minimizing waste and enabling sharing. SOSP2023 reports equal-latency throughput gains of 2–4×. Paging improves capacity but scatters request KV into many small segments, impairing CPU–GPU transfers. SuperInfer retains paging while adding block-first physical layouts/merged kernels:preserve logical paging, reorganize physical movement。

10.2 Sarathi-Serve: stall-free batching instead of prefill blocking decode

Chunked prefill/stall-free scheduling reduce interference; OSDI 2024 reports single-A100 Mistral-7B capacity 2.6×, Yi-34B on two A100s of 3.7×、Falcon-180B pipeline parallel 5.6×. It assumes request KV fits GPU memory; chunking cannot admit requests when HBM is full. SuperInfer retains chunking and proactively rotates residency:make iterations composable, then residency rotatable。

10.3 LTR: predicted SJF instead of FCFS

LTR learns relative output-length ordering rather than exact lengths. NeurIPS 2024 reports chatbot latency reductions up to 2.8×and synthetic throughput gains of 6.5×. It needs an offline ranker and cannot overcome exhausted HBM through queue order alone. SuperInfer uses observable waiting/last-token times and can actually evict running requests:replace predicted workload with measured SLO debt。

10.4 NEO: CPU–GPU computation beyond transfers

NEO offloads some attention with KV to CPU, using asymmetric pipelines/load-aware scheduling to expand batches. Abstract T4/A10G/H100 peak gains are 7.5× / 26% / 14%, with stronger CPUs giving A10G up to 79.3%. CPU enters the compute critical path, complicating hardware balance/model support; the baseline cannot run Mixtral-8x7B. SuperInfer keeps computation on Hopper and uses CPU only as a large KV tier:retain heterogeneous pipelines but narrow CPU responsibilities to reduce model coupling。

10.5 Pie: performance-transparent Superchip memory pooling

Pie is closest: predictable accesses/concurrent swapping on GH200 produce reported peak vLLM-relative throughput 1.9×, latency 2×, and equal-performance GPU-memory reduction 1.67×, with FlexGen-relative throughput 9.4×. Pie emphasizes capacity/performance transparency without explicit TTFT/TBT urgency or high-load proactive rotation. SuperInfer turns the CPU pool into an SLO-controlled execution tier, but lacks measured Pie head-to-head results because code was unavailable(§5.2).

11. Genuine contributions, reusable design, and transfer potential

Three actual contributions. First,scheduling abstraction: rotary states/VLT unify progress, first response, and continuous response into debt, transferable to SSD KV tiers, remote pools, and disaggregated clusters. Second,layout–protocol co-design: eager synchronization separates mutable tails from immutable full blocks, eliminating duplex races at protocol level. This extends to activation offloading, checkpoint streaming, and embedding caches. Third,awareness of physical limits: using192+192GB/s DRAM rather than advertised900GB/s as the ideal baseline is a sound evaluation practice.

Transfer is not free. MI300A differs in coherence, NUMA, copy engines, and bandwidth, requiring segment/concurrency retuning. PCIe systems may reuse VLT but cannot assume large B_xfer or DuplexKV throughput. Distributed rotary states add RTT/reliability to VLT. Combining lossy compression adds quality-loss budgets beyond time.

12. Stated boundaries and independent critique

12.1 Stated/directly identifiable boundaries

  1. Parameters are not automatically predicted from workloads. §5.3.2(PDF p.11) offers qualitative behavior only;(,_B,_F) require application customization, without online-query-distribution prediction.
  2. Benefits vanish at low load. Introduction(PDF p.2) states that with ample memory SuperInfer matches baselines. Its value is SLO-aware memory management under pressure, not universal per-token kernel acceleration.
  3. Code validation covers one GH200. README’s AE setup uses one GH200(144GB HBM,480GB DRAM); other Ampere/Hopper cards should work but are untested. TP=2 experiments still use related GH200/NVLink hardware.

12.2 Independent critique(evidence→implication)

  1. [External validity] Because main experiments use only GH200 NVL2 and a fixed 400GB pool,74.7 pp cannot generalize to PCIe, MI300A, CXL, or heterogeneous cloud instances with different feasible B_xfer ranges.
  2. [Baseline fairness] Because Pie is excluded for unavailable code despite being the closest GH200 pooling approach, evidence establishes superiority only over implemented baselines, not the nearest contemporary Superchip system.
  3. [Statistical reliability] Because Figures 16–23 lack repeats/error bars/CIs/tests, maximum+74.7 pp may amplify load-transition/runtime noise. Request-level bootstrap and repeated trace-replay intervals are needed.
  4. [Threshold dependence] Because main SLOs are TTFT5s/TBT100ms and the quality of(,_B=0,_F=0.5) depends directly on thresholds, balance is not intrinsic. If interactive voice requires TBT<50ms, rankings/transitions may change.
  5. [Workload realism] Because Poisson-driven ShareGPT/LMSYS misses bursts, daily cycles, priorities, shared prefixes, and retry correlations, production-burst recovery is unproven. Self-exciting arrivals and real-trace replay are needed.
  6. [Contribution attribution] Because merging64KB segments into 4MB alone cuts1556.15→159.87ms(9.73×), while full DuplexKV cuts63.14→46.80ms(1.35×), layout/batching drive the largest gains. They should not all be attributed to duplex transfers or scheduling.
  7. [Cost/energy] Because only SLOs, throughput, and latency are reported—not GH200 price, CPU/DRAM power, energy per million tokens, or TCO against two H100s—economic superiority of large-DRAM Superchips with 400GB KV pools is unknown.
  8. [Multitenant fairness] Because LVF prioritizes maximum debt without per-tenant slowdown, starvation, or Jain fairness measurements, combinations of priorities, long outputs, and frequent restoration may monopolize service. Tenant weights and maximum consecutive rotations should be explicit.

13. Open challenges: actionable head-to-head experiment gaps

Theory

  • VLT vs. deadline/queueing optimum： Under equal traces, HBM/DRAM, and transfer budgets, compare LVF, EDF, Whittle-index, and estimated SRPT on both SLO violations, worst-case starvation, and competitive ratio. Existing curves do not establish when piecewise VLT approximates optimality.
  • Fixed parameters versus online control: On stationary Poisson, Markov-burst, and self-exciting Hawkes arrivals, compare fixed(,), bandit controllers, and MPC, preregistering weighted TTFT/TBT violations plus parameter-switch costs.
  • Lossless offloading versus lossy compression: At equal HBM+DRAM byte budgets, compare SuperInfer, KV quantization/eviction, and combinations. Add perplexity, task accuracy, and exact-output agreement to SLOs, forming quality–capacity–tail-latency frontiers.

Engineering applications

  • SuperInfer vs. Pie： Run identical ShareGPT/LMSYS traces on the same GH200, vLLM commit, and 400GB pool. Report TTFT/TBT CDFs, throughput, C2C/DRAM bandwidth, CPU use, power, and 30 repeats. This is the key missing comparison.
  • GH200 vs. MI300A vs. PCIe H200： Fix models/traces, tune segment sizes/B_xfer separately, and compare fractions of platform-specific physical limits rather than raw GB/s.
  • One versus 8 tenants: Assign different SLOs/weights, compare LVF/weighted-LVF, and report per-tenant attainment, P99 slowdown, and starvation to test production QoS.

Emerging directions

  • Reasoning models with long CoT: Compare SuperInfer, SkipKV, and combinations on DeepSeek/Qwen reasoning at fixed accuracy/max tokens, testing complementarity or interference between moving full KV and suppressing redundant generation.
  • prefill/decode disaggregation： Place rotary tiers in decode pools, remote KV servers, or CXL memory, comparing local GH200. Measure restoration latency under congestion and failure recovery to test cross-node VLT.
  • Agents and shared multi-turn context: Compare ContextPilot/prefix caching at equal reuse rates, assessing avoided prefill versus proactive rotation, marginal gains, and cache pollution in long conversations.

14. Recent-work radar

The following come from visited official MLSys 2026 pages for future tracking, not deep readings here:

  1. FlashAttention-4: redesigns pipelines for asymmetric Blackwell scaling, achieving up to 1.3×over cuDNN9.13 on B200 and reaching 1613 TFLOPs/s / 71% utilization; operator optimization complements SuperInfer residency/SLO management.
  2. ContextPilot: context indexing/alignment/deduplication accelerates long-context prefill by up to 3×while preserving quality; worth combining with rotation/offloading.
  3. MorphServe: swaps quantized layers at runtime and changes KV capacity, reducing mean SLO violations by 92.45%and improving P95 TTFT by 2.2–3.9×, but adds a quality dimension requiring equal-quality comparisons.
  4. SkipKV: sentence deduplication/steering reduce storage and generation, reaching throughput 1.7×and sequences 1.6× shorter. It changes model trajectories, not a lossless SuperInfer substitute.
  5. BatchLLM: global prefix sharing and throughput-oriented token batching yield offline gains over vLLM/SGLang of 1.3–10.8×, reminding us online SLO conclusions do not directly transfer to batch serving.
  6. Optimizing Deployment Configurations for LLM Inference: draws on millions of configuration searches for Meta’s nearly billion monthly users, emphasizing phase-specific parallelism, heterogeneous hardware, and MoE effects. SuperInfer belongs in that broader deployment search space.

15. Conclusion

SuperInfer offers a coherent co-design path: identify the two-order-of-magnitude gap between advertised C2C and software performance, improve layouts to cut16GB bidirectional KV movement from 1556.15 ms to 46.80 ms, then convert bandwidth into proactive TTFT/TBT rotation through VLT/LVF. Evidence supports alleviating HOL blocking on GH200 under fixed SLOs/high load, not universal serving optimality. Pie remains unmeasured, plots lack tables/statistical intervals, and hardware/workload generalization is narrow. The reusable lesson is:proactive rotation helps scheduling only when transfers approach physical limits and overlap compute; otherwise it merely moves HOL blocking from waiting to swap queues.
