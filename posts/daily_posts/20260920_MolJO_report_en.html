2. Motivation: why improving molecules is harder than generating them

Structure-based generation combines continuous 3D coordinates and discrete atom types under competing objectives. Better Vina affinity need not improve QED, SA, or connectivity. Coordinate-only gradients may move atoms to proxy-friendly positions without appropriate type changes; hard argmax breaks gradients; RL or generate–filter–retrain loops increase search costs rapidly.

Under one CrossDocked2020 protocol, gradient-guided TAGMol has Success Rate 11.1%; stronger iterative-search DecompOpt has 39.4%; MolJO reaches 51.3%(main Table 1, HTML lines 279–297). Versus TAGMol, absolute gain is 40.2 ppand relative gain 362.16%; versus strongest same-protocol DecompOpt, 11.9 ppand 30.20%. Beyond more negative Vina, MolJO improves DecompOpt Vina Dock −7.63→−9.05, 1.42 kcal/mol in magnitude (+18.61%); SA 0.73→0.78 (+0.05, +6.85%), with QED tied at 0.56.

This is multiobjective, not one leaderboard. MolJO Diversity 0.66 trails TAGMol 0.69/IPDiff 0.74 but exceeds DecompOpt 0.63. Selecting the best of ten samples per pocket raises MolJO† success from 51.3% to 70.3%while diversity falls 0.66→ 0.61(Table 1): +19.0 pp/+37.04% success, −0.05/−7.58% diversity. Search budget contributes materially; top-of-ten and single-sample quality cannot be conflated.

3. Argument, core assumptions, and trade-offs

Four steps define the method. Inherit MolCRAFT's BFN, representing types as probabilities and coordinates as Gaussian parameters—a continuous belief state behind discrete samples. Train affinity/QED/SA energy predictors and jointly differentiate coordinate/type parameters, unlike TAGMol's explicit-coordinate/implicit-type guidance. Use sliding-window backward correction over the latest k states to escape premature local guidance and balance exploration/exploitation. Prove SE(3)-equivariant coordinate guidance and extend to R-group redesign, fragment growing, scaffold hopping, joint objectives, and large-pocket OOD.

Three assumptions matter: energy-predictor (E_phi( heta,t,p)) gradients near BFN trajectories align with true property improvements, though validation uses proxies, not wet labs; probability gradients translate into useful discrete type choices despite categorical sampling; and backward windows find better basins without destroying validity. Appendix k sweeps support an empirical stable region, not global optimality.

Three tradeoffs are central:Properties versus validity: stronger affinity guidance can reduce connectivity;Exploitation versus exploration: short B.C. windows accumulate local error, long windows add rollback cost;Quality versus budget: top-of-ten boosts success but no longer matches single-sample cost. The appendix exposes these transitions, though the abstract's SOTA claim does not state their boundaries alongside it.

4. Equation-level formalization and algorithm

4.1 Differentiable BFN parameter space

For data (x) and latent parameters ( heta), BFN maintains sender, Bayesian-update, and output distributions over time (t). Eqs.1–3 (HTML lines 145–155) update parameters after noisy message (y):

[ p_F(x;t)=E_{yp_S(yx;(t))},!(-h(_0,y,(t))), ]

A neural network ((,t,p)) predicts clean data or the next distribution, conditioned on pocket p. Coordinates use Gaussian means/precisions; types use categorical probabilities. Discrete atoms are not pretended continuous: derivatives act on the probability simplex before sampling.

4.2 Joint gradient guidance

Given property energy (E(,t,p)) and scale s, Eqs.4–5 (lines 170–177) approximate conditioned transitions through exponential tilting:

[ p(‘,p,E)p(’,p). ]

For coordinates, Eqs.6–7 (lines 180–189) shift means along (-_{}E); for types, Eqs.8–9 (lines 191–203) adjust probabilities and renormalize. Energy uses SE(3)-invariant/equivariant coordinate representations, preserving rotation/translation equivariance under Proposition 4.4 (lines 204–210). Multiobjective gradients are simply averaged—interpretable but not adaptive Pareto weighting.

4.3 Backward correction

Single-step guidance propagates current errors. At step i, B.C. stores history, revisits the latest k steps, resamples historical posteriors, and corrects with current gradients. Eqs.11–14 (lines 243–258) summarize as

[ i^{} =!({i-k}, y_{i-k+1:i}, -s_E(_i,t_i,p)), ]

The discrete implementation substitutes approximate derivative ((1-)) for numerical stability. This is an engineering heuristic, not uniquely implied by the exact categorical Jacobian, and a key gap between effectiveness and theoretical completeness to reproduce.

4.4 Pseudocode

Input: protein pocket p; pretrained MolCRAFT; property energy Eφ; n=200 steps; scale s=50; window k=130
Initialize coordinate belief θx,0, type probabilities θv,0; history H←∅
for i = 1..n:
    t ← i/n
    x̂, v̂ ← Φ(θx,i-1, θv,i-1, t, p)
    gx ← ∂Eφ/∂θx ; gv ← ∂Eφ/∂θv
    θx,i ← ContinuousBFNUpdate(θx,i-1, x̂, -s·gx)
    θv,i ← DiscreteBFNUpdate(θv,i-1, v̂, -s·gv)
    H.push(θx,i, θv,i)
    if correction step:
        j ← max(0, i-k)
        (θx,i, θv,i) ← ReconstructAndResample(H[j:i], gx, gv)
Sample final atom types, decode coordinates, check connectivity/chemical validity
Output: candidate molecules; optionally generate ten per pocket and select by success criteria

6. Datasets, benchmarks, and metrics

CrossDocked2020 aligns protein pockets/ligand complexes and is common in SBDD/SBMO. Evaluation covers de novo optimization, R-group redesign, fragment growing, scaffold hopping, and large-pocket OOD, plus joint objectives, geometry, and timing. Vina Score typically evaluates unoptimized poses, Vina Min applies local minimization, and Vina Dock performs docking search—distinct variables. QED combines empirical drug-likeness, SA proxies synthesizability, Diversity measures variation, and Success is the conjunction of three thresholds.

These metrics compare generation strategies under the same docking/proxy pipeline, not clinical activity, selectivity, toxicity, ADMET, synthetic yield, or binding free energy. Docking often improves with molecular size; despite main-experiment atom-count controls, large-pocket OOD results require checking average atom counts.

11. Related work: problem origins and technical transfer

11.1 Bayesian Flow Networks: unified parameter space

Problem solved. BFNs express noise and generation through Bayesian updates in distribution-parameter space, unifying continuous/discrete variables (arXiv:2308.07037).
Remaining gap. Original BFN addresses generation, not pocket-conditioned joint coordinate/type optimization through properties.
MolJO's response. Train/differentiate energies on intermediate beliefs, avoiding gradients through final hard type sampling.
Design transfer. Mapping heterogeneous modalities into differentiable belief parameters before joint guidance transfers to protein sequence–structure, material composition–lattice, and reactant–pathway generation.

11.2 MolCRAFT: unified generation to controllable optimization

Problem solved. MolCRAFT jointly generates coordinates/types in continuous parameter space. The ICML 2024 page reports reference-level Vina Score −6.59 kcal/mol, improving a strong baseline by 0.84.
Remaining gap. It is a pocket-conditioned generator, not a direct inference-time interface for higher affinity/QED/SA.
MolJO's response. Reuse the pretrained backbone and train only property predictors, isolating controller gains rather than a larger generator.
Design transfer. Pretrain a feasible-domain model, then attach small differentiable property heads: often more economical than full AI4Science retraining.

11.3 TAGMol: guided coordinates, lagging discrete types

Problem solved. TAGMol adds property gradients to diffusion sampling. Table 1 reports Vina Score −7.02, Min −7.95, Dock −8.59, QED 0.55, SA 0.56, Diversity 0.69, establishing test-time optimization without RL.
Remaining gap. Guidance explicitly acts on coordinates; types respond through generator coupling, and SA remains low. Hit/success definitions also differ between papers.
MolJO's response. Direct type-probability gradients plus B.C. prevent accumulating local errors. Under the core paper's common protocol, TAGMol success is 11.1% versus MolJO 51.3%.
Design transfer. Mixed-modal optimization must address more than the easily differentiated half; ablate every modality against every objective.

11.4 DecompOpt: strong but expensive black-box search

Problem solved. DecompOpt combines decomposed generation, property filtering, and iterative optimization. With Opt Prior, its original reports Success 52.5%, Vina Dock −8.98, Diversity 0.60 over thirty rounds.
Remaining gap. Repeated retraining/filtering costs 11714±1115 s in MolJO Table 16's remeasurement; no universal multiobjective optimum emerges.
MolJO's response. Differentiable optimization within one 200-step trajectory takes 146±11 s. Under MolJO's unified protocol, DecompOpt success is 39.4% versus MolJO 51.3%.
Design transfer. When scientific proxies are differentiable or distillable, trajectory control can be cheaper than outer-loop search. Unreliable surrogates still require black-box verification.

12. Genuine contributions, reusable design, and transfer potential

Contribution one: an interface for discrete guidance. The key is retaining categorical types as probabilities until final sampling, so gradients change what atoms are, not only where they are. This suits mixed continuous–discrete scientific objects.

Contribution two: B.C. is an optimizer, not a sampling trick. Vanilla+gradient barely helps, SDE+gradient helps partly, and B.C.+gradient improves all five metrics. Scientific test-time guidance needs explicit exploration/error recovery.

Contribution three: practical efficiency. An 80.23× single-GPU time advantage over DecompOpt replaces expensive per-pocket adaptation with one controlled sample using shared backbone/property heads. This matters for screening scale beyond one mean metric.

Transfers include amino-acid types plus backbone coordinates, crystal element occupancy plus lattice coordinates, and reaction templates plus conditions/barriers. All need differentiable parameterization, proxy uncertainty, and B.C.-like exploration/recovery—not just copying a gradient update.

13. Author-stated limitations

  1. Multiobjective conflict (joint-objective appendix, lines 679–681). The authors note QED+SA guidance can hurt affinity and use simple averaging. Uncalibrated gradient units/angles mean an empirical compromise, not guaranteed Pareto optimality.
  2. Window error and hyperparameter dependence (Eqs.11–14; appendix lines 744–750). Small k accumulates errors; 130 is selected. Without cross-step-count/backbone tables, stability applies only to the current 200-step CrossDocked setting.
  3. Evaluation and potential misuse (conformation lines 795–797; Impact lines 371–373). The authors acknowledge Auto-Dock pose/RMSD limitations and potential harmful-substance design requiring wet-lab resources. All main conclusions remain computational; real safety/activity are unverified.

14. Independent critique: evidence-defined boundaries

  1. [Construct validity] Becauseclaims rely on Auto-Dock Vina, QED, and SA without assays, free-energy perturbation, or synthesis,therefore51.3% Success means passing three computational thresholds, not proven binding, selectivity, toxicity, or synthetic yield.
  2. [Proxy rewards/OOD optimization] Because scale 50→100 improves Vina Min −8.39→−9.87 while Connected falls 0.90→0.55 and SA 0.68→0.63 (Table 13),thereforeenergy guidance has a reward-hacking region where better proxies leave chemical feasibility.
  3. [Size confounding] Becauselarge-pocket MolJO averages 30.0 atoms versus Reference 22.8 (+31.58%), and docking depends on size/contact area,therefore−10.53 cannot be credited entirely to better chemistry. Add ligand efficiency, per-heavy-atom scores, and atom-count-matched controls.
  4. [Search-budget fairness] BecauseMolJO† selects among ten candidates/pocket, raising success 51.3→70.3% and reducing diversity 0.66→0.61 without matched baseline oracle calls,therefore70.3% is not equal-budget SOTA; plot success versus oracle calls.
  5. [Statistical completeness] Becauset-tests/CIs cover selected continuous metrics, not success, constrained tasks, OOD, or top-of-ten, with no multiple-testing correction,thereforestable continuous-score differences do not prove all success gaps exceed between-pocket variability.
  6. [Consistency/reproduction] BecauseTable 1 and Appendix Table 6 disagree on some DecompOpt/MolCRAFT means, such as DecompOpt Vina Score −5.75 versus −4.839, and README examples use fifteen epochs versus at most five for property heads in the paper,thereforereproduction must pin the table, checkpoint, and command rather than assume repository defaults match the paper.
  7. [Discrete-gradient theory] Becausethe implementation uses ((1-)) derivative approximations for stability without bias/convergence bounds,thereforejoint discrete guidance is effective engineering, not a theoretically complete optimal update. Compare exact simplex Jacobians, Gumbel-softmax, and straight-through estimators at equal budgets.
  8. [Extrapolation] Becausetraining/main testing uses CrossDocked2020's 100,000 poses/100 proteins, and 30% sequence similarity does not audit pocket/scaffold leakage,thereforeeffectiveness across target families, covalent ligands, metal pockets, or flexible proteins is unestablished.

15. Open challenges: executable head-to-head experiments

15.1 Theory

  1. Discrete-gradient estimators. Fix MolCRAFT, property heads, 200 steps, samples/pocket, and seeds; compare ((1-)), exact simplex Jacobian, Gumbel-softmax, and straight-through updates. Report properties, connectivity, gradient variance, time, and final type flips. Estimator-specific B.C. effects would separate rollback from approximation benefits.
  2. Budget-equivalent B.C. Give all methods 200 energy/network calls; compare B.C. k∈{25,50,100,130,200}, SDE, multistart beam, and MCMC rejuvenation. Use success–calls AUC rather than only endpoints.
  3. Multiobjective Pareto mechanisms. At fixed total gradient norm, compare averaging, GradNorm, MGDA, PCGrad, and uncertainty weighting. Report affinity/QED/SA hypervolume, connectivity, and worst objective regressions to assess averaging.

15.2 Engineering and applications

  1. Blinded proxy/physical validation. On one pocket/scaffold split, have MolJO/TAGMol/DecompOpt submit fixed candidate counts, rerank with unseen docking engines/FEP, then blindly assay top candidates. Calibrate Vina gains against real K_d/IC50 gains.
  2. Size-matched controls. Match heavy atoms, formal charge, and rotatable bonds across methods; report Vina/heavy atom, ligand efficiency, QED, SA, and success to isolate size's contribution to OOD −10.53.
  3. Full-cost audit. On identical GPU/CPU and target successes/pocket, include predictor training, sampling, docking, top-N selection, retries, and I/O. Report energy, P50/P95 time-to-valid, and GPU-hours per successful molecule.

15.3 Emerging directions

  1. Uncertainty-aware guidance. Add ensemble/conformal uncertainty to property heads and attenuate scales in uncertain regions. Compare fixed 50/100 to test retaining roughly −9 docking without scale-100 connectivity collapse to 0.55.
  2. Joint synthesis-route generation. Combine MolJO with CGFlow-like molecule/route design. Compare SA-only guidance and explicit reaction paths using real synthesis success, steps, cost, yield, and property Pareto surfaces. Explicit routes improving only proxies would falsify the benefit.
  3. Protein-flexibility feedback. At fixed ligand budgets, compare rigid-pocket MolJO, side-chain ensembles, induced-fit docking, and short MD/FEP rescoring by protein family. Test real complementarity versus overadaptation to rigid pockets.

16. Six recent works to track

  1. MolPilot / Piloting Structure-Based Drug Design via Modality-Specific Optimal Schedule（ICML 2025）：PMLR｜arXiv:2505.07286. Optimizes mixed-modal noise schedules rather than property gradients, reporting 95.9% PoseBusters pass; potentially a stronger backbone for steadier MolJO guidance.
  2. Compositional Flows for 3D Molecule and Synthesis Pathway Co-design（ICML 2025）：arXiv:2504.08051｜Code. Joint molecular/synthesis-path compositional flows report 4.2× sampling efficiency, directly addressing SA as a proxy rather than route evidence.
  3. LDMol: Text-Conditioned Molecule Diffusion Model Leveraging Chemically Informative Latent Space（ICML 2025）：PMLR｜arXiv:2405.17829. Language-conditioned chemical latents complement pocket-conditioned MolJO, testing whether natural-language goals yield reliable joint gradients.
  4. Scalable Non-Equivariant 3D Molecule Generation via Rotational Alignment（ICML 2025）：PMLR｜arXiv:2506.10186. Challenges the cost of enforcing equivariance throughout; compare accuracy/throughput against MolJO's SE(3) guarantees.
  5. Multi-Fidelity Latent Active Learning for Molecular Property Discovery（ICML 2025）：PMLR. Official materials report 50% mean binding-free-energy improvement. Multifidelity active learning could allocate budgets between docking surrogates and expensive physical validation.
  6. Hierarchical Graph Tokenization for Molecule-Language Alignment（ICML 2025）：arXiv:2406.14021｜OpenReview. Hierarchical graph tokens align molecules/language; connecting them to type parameters could test whether semantic edits are more stable than atomwise gradients.

17. Conclusion

MolJO's reusable contribution is the control chain: BFN unifies coordinate/type beliefs, joint gradients provide direction, and backward correction recovers from local errors. Main results, type/coordinate ablations, and the 80.23× DecompOpt time advantage support its effectiveness.

The scientific conclusion is narrower: MolJO advancesstructured molecular optimization on computational proxieswithout crossing the proxy-to-experiment gap. At scale 100, connectivity collapses 0.90→0.55: pleasing evaluators can improve scores while degrading molecules. Prioritize uncertainty, budget matching, size controls, and blinded experiments over another Vina record.
