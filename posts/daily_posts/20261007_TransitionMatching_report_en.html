2. Motivation: Each Step Can Do Only Something Simple

Diffusion and flow matching both divide the complex noise-to-data mapping into many nearly infinitesimal local updates. This simplifies local conditional distributions and stabilizes training, but repeatedly invokes an expensive backbone during sampling. The core paper controls for the same 1.7B DiT, 350 million licensed image–text pairs, and 500,000 training steps. Standard FM at its best-quality setting needs 128 large-backbone forward passes, taking on one H100 10.8 seconds; DTM uses 16 backbone passes plus internal solving in a small head, taking 1.6 seconds, a 6.75× speedup and latency reduction of 85.19%(Table 3, §4.2).

A second bottleneck is discretization and causal ordering in visual autoregression. Fluid reports reconstruction PSNR of 31.5for continuous tokens versus 26.6for discrete tokens; its 10.5B random-order continuous AR achieves COCO-30K FID of 6.16、GenEval 0.69(Fluid Figure 4, abstract, and §5). Raster-order causal models cannot revisit and rearrange global composition, while masked AR supports bidirectionality but does not naturally fit language models' causal architecture. TM therefore asks:Can continuous states and expressive generative kernels be retained while expressing generation as finitely many trainable probabilistic transitions, each potentially stochastic, nondeterministic, or fully causal?

The tradeoff is explicit: if each transition kernel is a small generative model, each large step requires internal integration; ARTM/FHTM also generate tokens individually, requiring transition_steps × image_tokens backbone passes. The paper therefore offers three routes rather than a universal algorithm: DTM prioritizes speed; ARTM/FHTM prioritize causal unification.

3. Narrative and hypotheses

The argument has four steps.

  1. Replace vector fields with transition kernels. Given source distribution p0 and data distribution pT, learn pθ(x_{t+1}|x_t)so that starting from X0~p0 and sampling sequentially T times yields XT~pT(Equation 1).
  2. Use a supervising process q to provide adjacent-state pairs. It requires only q0=p0; the process may be discontinuous, stochastic, or cross-resolution, offering more freedom than fixed SDE/ODE paths (Equations 2–5).
  3. Replace intractable transition kernels with latent-variable posteriors. Introduce Ythrough the law of total probability: first sample from the model Y~pθ(Y|Xt), then update using the known q(x_{t+1}|x_t,Y) (Equations 6–7).
  4. Instantiate three kernels. DTM predicts the global difference Y=XT−X0; ARTM predicts the next state token by token; FHTM conditions on the full history, producing a fully causal visual generator.

The central hypothesis is that at finite step sizes,q(Y|Xt) retains multimodality erased by FM's conditional mean; even a small head generating token-independent differences can compensate with a few outer steps. Theorem 1 only proves convergence of DTM to Euler FM as step size vanishes, not finite-step superiority. The claim that stochastic kernels outperform deterministic means rests mainly on experiments and subsequent theory.

4. Equations and pseudocode

4.1 General TM objective

The supervising process is defined by data-sample endpoints:

q(x0,…,xT)=q(x0,…,x_{T−1}|xT) pT(xT), q0=p0。

The ideal objective directly matches conditional transitions:

L(θ)=E_{t,Xt} D(q_{t+1|t}(·|Xt), pθ_{t+1|t}(·|Xt))。

When the distance D has a single-sample empirical form D̂ , regression can use (Xt,X_{t+1})~q :

L(θ)=E_{t,Xt,X_{t+1}} D̂(X_{t+1},pθ_{t+1|t}(·|Xt))(Equations 4–5).

Introducing latent variables further gives Y：

q(x_{t+1}|x_t)=∫q(x_{t+1}|x_t,y)q(y|x_t)dy，

and requires training only pθ(Y|Xt)≈q(Y|Xt). At sampling time, first generate Y and then apply an explicit update (Equations 6–7). Process design, kernel parameterization, and the within-kernel generative model become independently replaceable interfaces.

4.2 DTM: Stochastic differences rather than conditional means

Along the Cond-OT linear process:

Xt=(1−t/T)X0+(t/T)XT, X0~N(0,I), define Y=XT−X0。

Then:X_{t+1}=Xt+Y/T. Instead of outputting a single velocity, a conditional flow head samples from pθ(Y|t,Xt) . Its conditional mean satisfies

E[Y|Xt=x]=E[XT−X0|Xt=x]=u_t(x), exactly FM's marginal velocity (Equation 14). FM is thus DTM's infinitesimal deterministic limit, while finite-step DTM preserves conditional-distribution width.

4.3 ARTM and FHTM

ARTM uses an independent linear process with independent noise at each discrete time, X_{0,t}, while retaining the ordinary linear process's marginals:

Xt=(1−t/T)X_{0,t}+(t/T)XT(Equation 15).

The next state is then factorized by the chain rule:

q(X_{t+1}|Xt)=∏_i q(X_{t+1}^i|Xt,X_{t+1}^{<i})。

FHTM further expands conditioning to the entire history X0,…,Xt, allowing a standard causal Transformer backbone. Each generated token requires a causal forward pass, however, sacrificing DTM's parallel speed.

4.4 Pseudocode

Training:
  repeat
    Sample t ~ Uniform{0,…,T−1} and data XT ~ pT
    Sample (Xt, Y) | XT from supervising process q
    Update θ using empirical flow-matching loss D̂(Y, pθ(Y|t,Xt))

Sampling:
  X0 ~ p0
  for t = 0,…,T−1:
    Y ~ pθ(Y|t,Xt)              # Internal ODE in the small flow head
    X_{t+1} ~ q(·|Xt,Y)          # Xt + Y/T for DTM
  return XT

8. Layer-by-Layer Ablations and Attribution

8.1 Speed–quality transitions (Tables 8 / 9)

More TM steps are not always better for DTM. At head NFE=4, CLIPScore for 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 TM steps is 17.9、21.1、25.4、26.7、26.8, followed at 32/64/128 steps by 26.7/26.5/26.5; it saturates at 16 steps. PickScore likewise reaches about 21.1 around 16 steps. Increasing head NFE from 1 to 4 produces the largest jump, with minimal gains thereafter. The practical sweet spot is 16 outer steps × 4 head NFE, rather than the main tables' 32×64.

The H100 timing table gives approximately 84 ms/stepfor the backbone and 3.5 ms/stepfor the head. A rough decomposition of 16×4 is 16×84 + 16×4×3.5 = 1568 ms, consistent with Table 3's 1.6 s; 128-step FM takes about 10.8 s. Head cost is not negligible even at the sweet spot—approximately 224 ms, or 14.3%—but remains far below backbone cost.

8.2 Supervising processes: Same marginals, different conditional regularity

ARTM/FHTM degrade when using DTM's shared-X0 linear path, because the near-deterministic linear relationship between Xt and X_{t+1} permits a training shortcut that fails at inference (Appendix A.4, Figure 9). The independent linear process resamples X_{0,t}at each t, preserving marginals while widening conditional support and markedly improving the 100K-step training curve. Figure 9 lacks a complete numerical table, so no pp gain can be invented. Conditional coupling is established as a key variable, not a cross-dataset statistical effect size.

8.3 Kernel expressiveness: Larger patches help only at low step counts

DTM defaults to independent head tokens corresponding to 2×2×4 latent patches. Increasing patches to 2×8×4 helps at 1–4 TM steps, but the difference vanishes at larger counts (Figure 10). This matches Theorem 1's limiting intuition: small steps approach local deterministic flows, reducing the need for joint cross-token kernels. Figure 10 lacks a complete numeric matrix, preventing quantification of the larger-patch gain.

8.4 Scheduler (Table 10)

For ARTM-3/DiT, MS-COCO CLIP/Pick changes from uniform 25.9914/20.8412 to exponential 26.07/20.92; PartiPrompts changes from 26.8494/20.8204 to 27.02/20.87. FHTM-3/DiT also improves slightly on COCO, but FHTM-3/LLM's Parti CLIP falls from 26.9945 to 26.95. Scheduler effects are therefore around 0.05–0.17 without a consistent directional advantage.

9. Sensitivity, transitions, and significance

Two clear transitions appear. First, increasing DTM from 2 to 4 TM steps at head NFE=4 raises CLIP from 21.1 to 25.4, a gain of 4.3; 8 to 16 adds only 0.1, with no improvement beyond 16. Second, at 16 TM steps, increasing head NFE from 1 to 2 raises CLIP from 23.2 to 26.4, a gain of 3.2; 2 to 4 adds only 0.4. The bottleneck first concerns within-kernel generation accuracy, then outer-backbone approximation; both saturate quickly.

The appendix includes CFG-scale sensitivity plots, but the HTML provides no complete machine-readable values; main results fix scale=6.5. Training reports one configuration and one curve, withno multiple random seeds, standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests. DTM generally leads FM in the reported run, but MS-COCO Pick +0.08 or Aesthetic +0.096 cannot be called statistically significant.

10. Actual contributions, reusable designs, and transfer potential

The contribution is separating generation into three interfaces: supervising process q, latent-variable parameterization Y, and within-kernel generative model B|A. Diffusion, flow, and continuous AR become configurations of one training template, exposing previously coupled design freedoms.

The most reusable engineering design is a large backbone extracting semantics and a small head solving local stochastic kernels. When backbone latency dominates, more numerical solving can be assigned to a head with 2% of the parameters, exchanging cheap NFEs for expensive ones. This may transfer to video, 3D, molecules, or audio, but the head's local-independence assumption must be revalidated.

FHTM's potential lies in a unified multimodal causal model: text prefixes and continuous image tokens can share standard LLM attention, training, and caching. Current evidence only shows that a 1.7B LLM architecture performs competitively on several automatic image metrics; it does not test language understanding, image QA, or interleaved generation. Seamless multimodal integration remains an architectural compatibility claim, not a demonstrated capability.

11. Limitations acknowledged by the paper

  1. Speed limitations (§4.2, §5): ARTM/FHTM do not accelerate sampling; backbone passes equal transition steps times image-token count, which in Tables 1/7 is 2×256 or 3×256. Causal unification and sampling efficiency are separate routes.
  2. Kernel independence (§2.2, §4.2): DTM's head generates difference tokens independently, limiting joint expressiveness; larger patches help only at low TM step counts.
  3. Future work (§5): the authors explicitly leave schedulers, distillation, and integrating FHTM into multimodal systems to future work. Neither distillation nor a truly unified text–image model is completed here.

12. Independent critique (evidence → implications)

  1. [Measurement scope] The main-table NFE* counts only the 1.7B backbone and excludes 64 evaluations of the 40M head inside each DTM step. Thus, 32 versus 256 NFE does not imply 8× less total computation. Table 3's 10.8 s→1.6 s (6.75×) is a more credible systems metric.
  2. [Efficiency attribution] Table 8 shows near-saturation at 16×4, whereas the main tables use 32×64. Main quality and speed results therefore use different sampling configurations; not all main-table quality scores can be attributed to the 1.6 s setting.
  3. [Statistics] No main table reports seed counts, standard deviations, or CIs, and MS-COCO Pick improves by only 0.08 (0.38%). Such small differences may fall within training or sampling variation and cannot establish a stable advantage.
  4. [Data reproducibility] Training relies on 350 million unavailable licensed Shutterstock pairs, and no code or weights were found. Fairness from fixed data, architecture, and optimization is therefore internally controlled but externally unverifiable.
  5. [Metric coverage] The paper omits FID, precision/recall, blind human evaluation, and copying rates; its six Parti/COCO metrics are mostly learned rewards. The evidence supports improved proxy preferences, not comprehensive gains in coverage, originality, or human preference.
  6. [Compositionality boundaries] DTM's GenEval Overall exceeds FM by 7 pp, but Counting from 0.27 to 0.35 remains below FHTM-3's 0.44, and single-object performance trails MAR's 0.98. Claims of improved compositionality should be restricted to subcategories such as two objects, color, and position.
  7. [Theory–practice gap] Theorem 1 proves convergence to Euler FM only when T→∞ , while practical gains occur at finite T. It explains why the methods eventually coincide, not why finite steps are better. Subsequent AISTATS 2026 work adds KL advantages under Gaussian and locally unimodal Gaussian-mixture conditions.
  8. [Unified-model narrative] FHTM-3 LLM merely switches 2D positional encoding to 1D and uses a text prefix, without evaluating language generation or interleaved sequences. Experiments support architectural portability, not completed unification of text and image generation.

13. Related-Work Lineage: Problem → Technical Source → Response

13.1 Flow Matching（Lipman et al., 2022）

Problem addressed: regresses vector fields along conditional probability paths, avoiding simulation-based training and using Cond-OT for straighter, faster paths.Remaining gap: inference still solves a deterministic ODE through many expensive backbone calls; large finite steps collapse conditional multimodality into a mean.How the core paper responds: DTM does not merely regress E[Y|Xt], but samples the full p(Y|Xt)。Transferred design: retains Cond-OT linear paths, the velocity identity, and flow-matching heads, but uses flow matching as a within-kernel generator rather than the overall objective.

13.2 MAR：Autoregressive Image Generation without VQ（Li et al., 2024）

Problem addressed: models continuous tokens with a small diffusion loss, avoiding VQ information loss.Remaining gap: ordering and attention in single-round masked/raster AR constrain global composition, while within-token diffusion remains expensive.How the core paper responds: ARTM embeds autoregressive token generation within multiple outer Markov transitions; FHTM additionally retains the full history.Transferred design: directly inherits and extends the 1.7B backbone plus 6-layer small head, continuous latents, and per-token flow.

13.3 Fluid（Fan et al., 2024）

Problem addressed: systematically compares discrete/continuous tokens and raster/random order, showing stronger visual quality and GenEval from continuous random-order generation; 10.5B achieves COCO FID 6.16 and GenEval 0.69.Remaining gap: strong models rely on bidirectional masked attention, which does not naturally unify with causal LLMs.How the core paper responds: FHTM combines full history with causal attention, allowing a 1.7B LLM architecture to approach or exceed DiT on the authors' metrics.Transferred design: continuous tokens, random/causal order, and head NFE become comparison axes for TM.

13.4 Generator Matching（Holderrieth et al., 2025）

Problem addressed: unifies continuous and discrete generation through general Markov generators, including jump processes.Remaining gap: still starts from continuous-time infinitesimal generators, leaving implementations and model classes abstract.How the core paper responds: TM works directly with discrete-time transition kernels and trains on adjacent-state pairs, assigning expressiveness to the model inside each finite step.Transferred design: retains arbitrary supervising processes and modality-agnostic unification, while replacing the generator with a kernel as the modeling object.

13.5 Follow-up: Demystifying TM (AISTATS 2026)

Problem addressed: supplies finite-step theory missing from the original: for unimodal Gaussians, TM has lower KL at any finite step count because stochastic difference updates preserve covariance; advantages persist in local GMM regions with separated modes and nonzero variance.Remaining gap: conclusions rely on controlled distributional approximations and do not directly establish global optimality for large image models.Transferred design: advances the expressive-stochastic-kernel explanation from empirical attribution to covariance preservation, and releases image/video code.

14. Technical Evolution Tree

Continuous-time paths
├─ Diffusion: stochastic microsteps + high NFE
├─ Flow Matching: deterministic vector fields + ODE
│  ├─ Cond-OT: straighter paths
│  └─ Generator Matching: arbitrary continuous-time Markov generators
└─ Transition Matching: finite discrete steps + learnable probability kernels
   ├─ DTM: global stochastic differences → few expensive backbone calls + many cheap head calls
   │  └─ Demystifying TM: finite-step KL / covariance explanation
   ├─ ARTM: next-state autoregression over continuous tokens
   └─ FHTM: full-history causal Transformer
      └─ Goal: unified causal text–image model

Another technical lineage
VQ autoregression → MAR continuous tokens → Fluid random-order scaling
                         └────────────→ ARTM / FHTM

Three shifts drive this evolution: from simulating SDEs to directly regressing vector fields; from discrete VQ tokens to small generative heads for continuous tokens; and from deterministic updates to conditional distributions at every step. TM's key insight is that finite-step conditional distributions should retain variance, rather than merely using more complex paths.

16. Open Challenges Requiring Head-to-Head Tests

Theory

  1. Finite-step non-Gaussian bounds: with the same 1.7B backbone and data, compare DTM and FM at 2/4/8/16 steps on empirical KL surrogates, covariance spectra, and mode coverage. Preregister the prediction that DTM's advantage grows with mode separation, connecting AISTATS 2026 theory to real latents.
  2. Kernel-independence error: compare per-token, 2×2, 2×8, and full-image low-rank-coupled heads under fixed total FLOPs. Measure FID, precision/recall, and GenEval to separate larger joint kernels from merely more parameters.

Engineering applications

  1. Unified cost accounting: on H100/B200, report end-to-end latency, energy, peak memory, and all backbone/head NFEs for FM, DTM, MeanFlow, and Shortcut. Rank Pareto frontiers at matched CLIP/Pick scores.
  2. Reproducible fairness: retrain FM/DTM/MAR on a public DataComp-1B subset with at least 3 seeds, releasing weights and logs. Test whether +0.08 Pick exceeds the 95% CI.

17. Recent Work to Track

  1. Exploring the Design Space of Transition Matching(2025-12-13): trains 56 models at 1.7B and performs 549 evaluations to search heads, time weighting, and samplers, expanding the original's limited design points into an engineering map.https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12465
  2. Demystifying Transition Matching(AISTATS 2026): establishes conditions for finite-step TM superiority over FM on Gaussian/locally unimodal Gaussian mixtures, with official image/video code; the most direct mechanistic follow-up.https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17991
  3. Transition Matching Distillation for Fast Video Generation(2026-01-14): splits each outer transition into a backbone and flow head and distills Wan2.1 1.3B/14B, extending TM from training to pretrained video-model compression.https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09881
  4. Diff2Flow(2025-06-02): converts diffusion priors into flow matching through time and interpolation alignment for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Its migration of existing diffusion assets complements TM's redefinition of finite-step kernels.https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02221
  5. Vid2World(2025-05-20): causalizes pretrained video diffusion models and adds action guidance, an important comparison for TM in interactive world models.https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14357
  6. Co-generation of Layout and Shape via Autoregressive 3D Diffusion(2026-04-17): jointly generates layouts and object latents using 3D-ARD+ over 230,000 indoor scenes, illustrating the related outer-AR/inner-diffusion design in 3D.https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16552
  7. GaussianGPT(2026-03-27): quantizes Gaussian primitives with VQ for pure autoregression, providing a discrete 3D counterpart to TM's continuous states.https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26661

18. Conclusion

Transition Matching's central insight is:When sampling must use finite steps, the appropriate object may be an entire conditional transition distribution rather than a mean velocity. In the authors' controlled 1.7B comparison, DTM improves 5/6 PartiPrompts metrics over FM and GenEval Overall from 0.47 to 0.54, while H100 latency drops from 10.8 s to 1.6 s. This establishes stochastic finite-step kernels as a strong design space, not universal SOTA: training data and code are unavailable; FID, blind human evaluation, and multiple seeds are missing; main-table NFE* excludes the head; and FHTM's multimodal unification remains evaluated only through image-generation metrics.
