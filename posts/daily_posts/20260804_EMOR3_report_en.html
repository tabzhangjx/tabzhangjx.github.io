2. In-Depth Analysis of the Core Paper

2.1 Positioning in One Sentence

EMO-R3's claim: In subjective tasks, answer correctness does not constrain reasoning correctness. What reasoning should look like (structure) and whether it holds up (self-reflection) must become separate, scorable rewards, rather than expecting accuracy rewards to improve reasoning incidentally.

This is more than adding two rewards to GRPO. It is an explicit statement about the limits of RLVR: The justification for reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards rests on a tight coupling between reasoning and the final answer—an assumption that holds in mathematics but not emotion judgment. Where it fails, EMO-R3 constructs an alternative coupling channel.

Basic information. Yiyang Fang, Wenke Huang, Pei Fu, Yihao Yang, Kehua Su, Zhenbo Luo, Jian Luan, Mang Ye. School of Computer Science, Wuhan University + Xiaomi MiLM Plus. arXiv:2602.23802v1; CVPR 2026 Poster (2026-06-05, ExHall A-F), Award Candidate.

2.2 Motivation: Evidence Establishing the Problem

Most papers adding RL to a task motivate it by saying the task is important but difficult. EMO-R3 instead provides two specific, falsifiable observations and builds the entire paper around them.

Observation ❶: SFT Fails Because Accuracy Is Distributed Incorrectly, Not Simply Because It Is Too Low

The paper goes beyond qualitative claims. Table 1 offers striking evidence: the original Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct has AI=50.77, AO=45.71; after SFT, AI rises to 73.34(+22.57pp, a relative +44.46%), while AO collapses to 29.09(−16.62pp, a relative 36.36% decline).

The implication goes beyond mediocre SFT generalization: SFT makes the model 16.6 percentage points worse out of domain than doing nothing. An even more extreme result: after SFT on EmoSet, WebEmo accuracy is 17.75%—yet WebEmo has seven classes, giving random guessing an expected 14.29%. The SFT model exceeds chance by only 3.46pp. The paper attributes this to fixed, predefined labels locking the model into discrete emotion categories: SFT learns EmoSet's eight label words rather than emotion.

Observation ❷: A Correct Answer Does Not Imply Correct Reasoning—and the Paper Measures This

The experiment in Figure 1(b) deserves particular credit: the authors take the rollout's think passage, feed it back to the model in isolation, ask which emotion follows from the text, and compare that with the rollout's answer . The two frequently disagree.

This experiment is the paper's pivot. GRPO's effectiveness depends on an implicit assumption: Only correct reasoning reliably produces correct answers, so rewarding answers approximately rewards reasoning indirectly. This roughly holds on AIME, where one arithmetic mistake almost always invalidates the answer. It fails for “What emotion does this image evoke?”: a model can write irrelevant analysis and then guess a frequent label. The constraint imposed by accuracy rewards on reasoning therefore approaches zero, reducing GRPO to an expensive label classifier with formatting constraints.

What is new. The predecessor R1-Omni, in §5.2 “Hallucination in Reasoning,”already acknowledges this phenomenon but leaves it unresolved. DeepSeek-R1's limitations section anticipates a related risk from another angle: without reliable evaluation, the policy may find shortcuts that hack the reward model. EMO-R3 turns a problem identified but unaddressed by both lines of work into a technical problem with a constructive solution.

Why it matters. Since 2025, RLVR has spread much faster than its applicability conditions have been tested—from mathematics to code, video, GUIs, and emotion. EMO-R3 does something more general than improving emotion recognition: it offers a remedy for when verifiable rewards cease to be verifiable. Emotion is a particularly sharp test because of its strong subjectivity.

2.3 The Argument's Structure, Rather Than a Section-by-Section Retelling

EMO-R3 presents a four-step chain, with each step compelled by the previous one:

  1. Accuracy rewards cannot constrain reasoning in emotion tasks(measured in Figure 1(b)) → to obtain good reasoning, constraints must come from elsewhere.
  2. Constraints can target two orthogonal dimensions: form and content. Form specifies the steps reasoning should follow, enforceable through formatting rewards; content concerns whether those steps are valid, which formatting cannot enforce. This yields SET and RER.
  3. Form constraint = SET. Instead of allowing unrestricted chain-of-thought, the model must follow a three-stage emotion-reasoning template(identify triggers → consider human reactions → conclude valence/arousal), hard-coding appraisal theory into the format reward. SET's real role is less to teach thinking than to create two measurable interfaces for RER.
  4. Content constraint = RER. Since step 1 must describe the image, feed it back with the original image and ask whether the text describes that image → a visual-grounding signal. Since steps 1+2 must contain emotion reasoning, feed them back alone and ask which emotion best fits the text → a reasoning–answer consistency signal. The latter is precisely the reward version.
Three implicit design trade-offs worth highlighting, although the authors do not state them explicitly. ① Sacrifice reasoning diversity for evaluability(SET)—a drawback for creative tasks but a benefit for judgment tasks. ② Sacrifice evaluator independence for zero additional model cost(RER)—both rewards are scored by the policy model itself, saving memory while allowing evaluator and evaluated model to drift together (see §2.7, criticism 4). ③ Sacrifice training time for zero inference overhead—reflection occurs only during training; each step rises from ~600s to ~750s (about +25%), while deployment cost is unchanged.

2.4.4 Cold-Start-Emo: An Easily Misread Design

The paper explicitly states that Cold-Start-Emo is not intended to inject reasoning ability. It uses a small number of task-specific examples without CoT annotations to ease training difficulties caused by subjective bias, first teaching the model output formatting, the emotion-label system, and expression patterns.

This differs fundamentally from DeepSeek-R1's cold start, whose SFT data contain long reasoning chains to establish a favorable starting distribution for RL. EMO-R3's cold start is pure format alignment. This distinction determines how to read the ablation: the +0.34pp from Cold-Start-Emo in Table 2 should be interpreted as reducing formatting noise, rather than improving reasoning.

Increasing the sampling budget hurts both baselines while leaving EMO-R3 almost unchanged. A plausible explanation, untested in the paper, is: More rollouts increase the proportion of tied groups under binary accuracy rewards, where every reward is equal and every advantage is zero, diluting the gradient. 2.5.6 Hyperparameter Sensitivity

The paper provides no sensitivity sweep for λ₁ / λ₂. λ₁ = λ₂ = 0.1 is prescribed directly, without a curve for λ₁ ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3} or sweeps of β, learning rate, or temperature. The only hyperparameter trend is the two-point comparison G ∈ {4, 8}. This is the clearest gap in experimental completeness.

2.5.7 Statistical Significance

The paper states that each experiment runs three times to reduce randomness, reporting the median”. but no standard deviation, interquartile range, or confidence interval.

Test sets are restricted to 2,000 examples, so one classification flip = 0.05pp:

2.6 Assessment of Value

The Substantive Contribution

Beyond a 1.26pp improvement in emotion recognition, the contribution is a general remedy when verifiable rewards lose verifiability, with three separable components:

  1. Diagnostic paradigm: Make reasoning support for the answer measurable by removing the answer and image, retaining only reasoning, and testing answer recovery. Anyone using RLVR can use this to check whether a task satisfies its underlying assumptions.
  2. Structure as an interface: SET's value is less whether three stages are the optimal psychological process than that fixed reasoning slots become individually scorable objects. The general principle is: define a structure for reasoning before trying to score its process.
  3. Self-reflective rewards: Connect diagnostic metrics directly to reward terms. RER's specific form is emotion-specific, but constructing auxiliary rewards from diagnostics is domain-independent.

Independently Reusable Designs

Reusable componentTransfer formTarget setting

The fourth item deserves special emphasis. Many papers' generalization improvements are actually byproducts of in-domain gains, concealed by reporting only an overall mean. EMO-R3's separate AI / AO reporting exposes SFT's collapse and also makes its own gains transparent: +1.18pp is exactly +1.18pp. The methodological value of this protocol may exceed the algorithmic contribution.

Implications for Future Work

The most direct transfer opportunity is RL for human-preference tasks: Aesthetic scoring, content-appropriateness judgments, and empathetic response generation all have subjective answers weakly coupled to reasoning, and face the same RLVR failure as emotion tasks. The template applies directly: diagnose decoupling, define a task-specific reasoning structure, and design a self-reflective verifier for each slot.

More broadly, EMO-R3 suggests a potentially underappreciated direction: reward resolution may matter more than reward correctness. Moving from binary accuracy to a three-level (0/0.5/1) composite reward increases the gap from +0.53 to +1.26 at G=8. If this observation holds, increasing the resolution of sparse binary rewards is an independent research direction, regardless of the reflection narrative.

2.7 Limitations

Category I: Limitations Acknowledged by the Paper

A frank disclosure: The paper has no standalone Limitations section. Its conclusion mentions only future work on more complex multimodal settings, including sequential or interactive tasks. This itself invites criticism: omitting limitations from a CVPR Award Candidate falls short of current community norms. Three points are implicitly acknowledged in the method description:

  1. Cold start is required: Emotion understanding is highly subjective and context-dependent; sparse initial RL rewards necessitate Cold-Start-Emo. The method cannot train from scratch (§3.4).
  2. Higher training cost: RER requires additional forward queries, adding 25% per step (§4.5, explicitly disclosed).
  3. Reasoning can diverge from the answer: The authors note that individual or contextual differences can cause reasoning to depart from the final answer. This is both the motivation and a residual risk: RER mitigates but cannot eliminate it (§3.1).

Category II: Ten Independent Criticisms

Criticism 1 | Effect Size Cannot Be Separated from Noise(Argument gap + reproducibility risk)
The main +1.26pp gain at G=8 corresponds to approximately 25 examples; each ablation increment corresponds to 6–12 examples. The paper reports the median of three runs but no dispersion statistics. Given RL's inherently high variance, three-run medians cannot support causal attribution to two decimal places, such as SET adding 0.29pp and RER adding 0.58pp. This is the paper's weakest link: The evidence does not match the precision of the conclusions, even if the conclusions may be correct.

Criticism 2 | Ablations Use a Suboptimal Operating Point(Experimental design)
EMO-R3's best overall mean occurs at G=4 (A=60.50) rather than G=8 (60.42). Yet all ablations in Tables 2 and 3 use G=8. The stated component contribution ratio is therefore measured at a suboptimal hyperparameter setting, without checking whether it holds at G=4. Since the EMO-R3–GRPO gap differs by 2.4× between these settings, the relative contributions may also depend on G. This interaction is entirely unexplored.

Criticism 3 | “GRPO” Has Two Different Numbers in the Paper(Presentation)
Table 1's global GRPO(G=8) A = 59.16(six-column mean), whereas the GRPO baseline A in Tables 2/3 = 60.92(three-column mean for EmoSet training only). Both are correct and arithmetically verified, but table captions do not clarify the different scope. Subtracting 59.16 from 61.79 yields an incorrect +2.63pp gain; the actual gain is +0.87pp.

Criticism 4 | Policy Self-Evaluation Risks Reward Collusion, Without Controlled Comparisons(Argument gap + missing key comparison)
Both R_cons and R_coh are scored by M—the same MLLM being trained. Evaluator and policy drift together and are not independent. The model could learn step-1 text that it readily labels “Yes,” rather than text that accurately describes the image. No controlled experiment rules this out: There is no frozen-reference evaluator, no stronger third-party VLM evaluator, and no training-time distributions of R_cons/R_coh. If R_cons rapidly saturates near 1, it provides little signal. This is precisely the reward-hacking risk acknowledged by DeepSeek-R1. This is the most important head-to-head comparison to add.

Criticism 6 | DAPO Consistently Trails GRPO, Without Explanation(Validity of a key baseline comparison)
DAPO loses to GRPO under both rollout settings (58.28 vs 59.97; 56.91 vs 59.16). Its improvements—dynamic sampling, clip-higher, and token-level loss—typically help mathematical RL, so the reversal needs explanation. One possible mechanism is: DAPO's dynamic sampling filters out groups with identical rewards, which may be extremely common under binary rewards, sharply reducing the effective batch size. The paper neither diagnoses this nor states whether DAPO was separately tuned. Without explanation, this baseline resembles an untuned straw man.

Criticism 7 | Restricting Data to 2,000 Prevents Comparison with Leading Specialized Methods(Experimental design + missing key baselines)
EmoSet contains 118,102 images, but this study uses 2,000 training and 2,000 test examples. EmoVIT (CVPR 2024), by comparison, reports 83.36% on the full dataset, while this paper's highest EmoSet score is 77.81%. These figures cannot be directly compared, but the baseline table entirely omits the specialized emotion MLLMs EmoVIT, Emotion-LLaMA, and AffectGPT, including only general VLMs and RL variants. Readers cannot place EMO-R3 relative to the strongest specialized methods.

Criticism 8 | The Motivating Diagnostic Is Missing from the Results(Incomplete argument)
The paper is built on Figure 1(b)'s mismatch between the emotion inferred from the think passage and the final answer. Yet the post-training mismatch rate is never reported. All results measure classification accuracy. The central claim of improved reasoning–answer coupling is therefore never measured directly, only suggested by accuracy gains. One table could close this gap. Its absence leaves unclear whether +1.26pp comes from better reasoning or denser rewards.

Criticism 9 | No Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis(Reproducibility risk)
λ₁ = λ₂ = 0.1 comes without a sweep or justification. R_overall is a convex combination with accuracy weight 0.8. With only +1.26pp improvement, a sharp optimum caused by benchmark over-tuning cannot be excluded. A five-point λ₁ ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3} curve would cheaply turn a robustness claim into evidence.

Criticism 10 | Conclusions Rest on a Single 3B Base Model(External validity)
All experiments use Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, creating uncertainty in both directions: (a) SET's three stages may be necessary scaffolding for 3B but redundant constraints for 7B/32B models that already structure their reasoning spontaneously; (b) RER depends entirely on the base model's self-evaluation ability. Weak discrimination at 3B could underestimate gains through evaluator noise or overestimate them because external constraints help weaker models more. Without a second base model, method-specific findings cannot be separated from 3B-specific findings.

3. Revisiting Related Work

EMO-R3 brings together two previously separate research lines. The discussion below follows how problems pass between works, rather than listing them independently.

3.1 EmoVIT — 2024.06, CVPR 2024 (pp. 26596–26605)

① Problem addressed. By early 2024, visual instruction tuning was mature, but emotion-specific instruction data were missing, leaving general VLMs near chance on emotion tasks. EmoVIT's core contribution was a GPT-4-assisted data-synthesis pipeline followed by instruction tuning on InstructBLIP. Its key choice was to turn emotion understanding into a data problem. Headline result: 83.36% accuracy on EmoSet, versus BLIP2 46.79%, LLaVA 44.03%, InstructBLIP 42.20%, and Flamingo 29.59%, using only about half the training data.

② Unresolved gap. The 83.36% result comes from closed-set, single-dataset, static-image evaluation. Pure SFT offers no reasoning chain, policy learning, or cross-dataset generalization validation. Although there is no limitations section, the structural gaps are clear: (a) dataset-fixed labels, (b) a ceiling set by GPT-4 annotation quality, and (c) no interpretable output explaining the model's judgment. Most importantly, there is no zero-shot evaluation on other emotion datasets, so the score cannot distinguish learning emotion from learning EmoSet.

③ How the core paper responds. EMO-R3 turns this untested question directly into an experimental design. In its 2×3 cross-matrix, the SFT row quantitatively challenges the EmoVIT approach: 73.34 in-domain versus 29.09 out-of-domain. EMO-R3's protocol answers a question never asked of EmoVIT: The model learned the dataset.

④ Transfer of key designs. EmoVIT's positive legacy is the EmoSet dataset(118K images, eight classes), used by EMO-R3 for its primary training and evaluation after reduction to 2,000 examples. EMO-R3 also inherits and quantifies the view that emotion tasks need specialized adaptation and general VLMs perform poorly directly: vanilla Qwen2.5-VL-3B's AI is only 50.77.

3.2 Emotion-LLaMA — 2024.12, NeurIPS 2024

① Problem addressed. Unimodal methods could not capture real-world emotional complexity, while contemporary MLLMs could neither integrate audio nor recognize microexpressions. Two contributions followed: the MERR dataset(28,618 coarse-grained + 4,487 fine-grained examples, about 33K), and a model passing audio, vision, and text through emotion-specific encoders into an instruction-tuned LLaMA. Headline results: MER2023-SEMI F1 = 0.9036(A+V+T, Table 3); EMER Clue Overlap 7.83 / Label Overlap 6.25 (Table 1); zero-shot DFEW UAR 45.59 / WAR 59.37 (Table 2).

② Unresolved gap. The authors acknowledge that Neutral scores lowest among all classes because neutral emotion provides weak facial and audio cues, making automatic annotation harder. The MERR annotation pipeline thus fails precisely where cues are subtle. More structurally, modalities and datasets keep growing, but the paradigm remains unchanged: fitting an annotated distribution. When annotations are subjective and noisy, that fitting has an inherent ceiling.

③ How the core paper responds. The response changes the paradigm: optimize a policy instead of fitting a distribution. More subtly, Emotion-LLaMA struggles with weak cues, whereas SET's first step explicitly enumerates emotional triggers, turning cue detection from an implicit representation into a textual step that R_cons can score.

④ Transfer of key designs. The transfer is: from implicitly extracting emotional cues with specialized encoders to explicitly stating them in structured reasoning. The former hides cues in weights; the latter makes them natural-language text that can be challenged: does this text describe the image? This is the most important representational shift in this lineage.

3.3 AffectGPT — 2025.07, ICML 2025 Oral (PMLR v267: 36993–37014)

① Problem addressed. The work explicitly moves MER from simple discrimination toward complex emotion understanding. Three contributions: MER-Caption / MER-Caption+(115K samples, more than 2,000 fine-grained emotion categories), the AffectGPT model using pre-fusion , and the MER-UniBench unified benchmark. Headline result: MER-UniBench mean score 74.77(A+V+T), improving over previous MLLMs by roughly 9%(Table 2). Table 3's data ablation scores 74.77 when trained on MER-Caption+ versus 64.55 on MERR-Fine (Emotion-LLaMA's dataset), a 10.2pp gap.

② Unresolved gap. Three acknowledged limitations: restricted to single-person videos; model-generated MER-Caption+ annotations may remain inaccurate despite filtering; and, most critically, basic emotion recognition lacks fine-grained reference labels, making it impossible to fairly score a nonstandard but valid prediction. This is the inevitable problem of expanding eight classes to over 2,000: finer categories make correct answers less unique and exact-match evaluation less valid.

③ How the core paper responds. This is the most subtle handoff in the lineage. AffectGPT expands the label space to approximate emotional complexity, then hits the problem of undefined evaluative correctness. EMO-R3 takes the opposite direction: keep the label space small (6–8 classes) and broaden the definition of good performance—from correct answers to correct answers plus grounded, consistent reasoning. AffectGPT increases output-space resolution, while EMO-R3 increases reward-signal resolution.

④ Transfer of key designs. MER-UniBench establishes that evaluation should cover both classification metrics and free-text quality. RER can be seen as internalizing that principle in training: R_coh is effectively an online automated scorer of free-text quality.

3.4 DeepSeekMath / GRPO → DeepSeek-R1 — 2024.02 / 2025.09 (Nature 645:633–638)

① Problem addressed. DeepSeekMath Continued pretraining of DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 7B on 120B mathematical tokens introduces GRPO, a PPO variant removing the value/critic network and estimating the baseline with group-relative scores. Headline result: MATH 51.7%(without tools or voting), rising to 60.9% with 64-sample self-consistency voting. DeepSeek-R1 then demonstrates that reasoning can emerge from pure RL with no human reasoning traces(R1-Zero has no SFT cold start). Self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adjustment are all emergent behaviors. Headline result: AIME 2024 pass@1 = 79.8% (R1)/ 77.9% pass@1, 86.7% cons@16 (R1-Zero).

② Unresolved gap. GRPO was designed and validated entirely on mathematical reasoning with a single verifiable answer, without mechanisms for subjective tasks, multiple valid answers, or graded correctness. DeepSeek-R1's limitations anticipate the same problem from another direction: on tasks without reliable evaluation, the policy may exploit shortcuts to hack the reward model, motivating future work on defining and refining rewards for difficult-to-verify problems. Emotion is precisely such a domain.

③ How the core paper responds. The entire EMO-R3 paper can be read as a direct response to that DeepSeek-R1 limitation: Figure 1(b) confirms that shortcuts exist and are common in emotion tasks; RER refines reward structures for hard-to-verify problems, as R1 called for.

④ Transfer of key designs. EMO-R3 It retains GRPO's optimization side unchanged(Equation 1 is unmodified), operating only on the reward side. This deliberate positioning isolates the contribution along an orthogonal dimension, allowing it to combine with later GRPO variants such as DAPO or GSPO. It also inherits GRPO's problems, including degenerate group advantages under binary rewards, which I suspect explains weaker baselines at G=8.

3.5 R1-Omni — March 2025, arXiv Preprint (No Peer-Reviewed Publication)

① Problem addressed. This work from Tongyi Lab / Alibaba first applies RLVR to emotion recognition in an omni-modal LLM, jointly targeting reasoning, recognition accuracy, and generalization.

② Unresolved gap. ④ Transfer of key designs. Their settings differ considerably—video/audio/text with a 0.5B base versus static images with 3B—so independent validation in both strengthens the conclusion.

4.3 Two Key Turning Points

Turning Point 1: From Deterministic to Adaptive Systems (March 2025, R1-Omni). Previously, emotion MLLM behavior was determined by the training distribution. With RL, it is shaped by the reward function. The quantitative evidence is AO: 29.09 → 52.42 (+23.33pp, or 95.2% of the total out-of-domain gain). This is the lineage's only phase transition.

Turning Point 2: From Rewarding Outcomes to Rewarding Processes (February 2026, EMO-R3). Original RLVR rewards only final answers; EMO-R3 moves rewards upstream to reasoning itself. Its quantitative effect is much smaller (+1.18pp on AO, or 4.8%), but it changes the method's applicability boundary rather than merely a performance figure, allowing RL into domains with unverifiable outcomes.

4.4 Shifting Community Priorities

Early (first half of 2024): Accuracy. EmoVIT's 83.36% is a straightforward leaderboard result.
Intermediate (late 2024–2025): Coverage. Modalities, categories, and dataset size become central competitive dimensions (33K → 115K / 2000+ classes).
Current (late 2025–2026): Generalization and Interpretability. EMO-R3's protocol is organized around separate AI / AO reporting, while its two mechanisms target trustworthy reasoning rather than accuracy directly.

This shift accompanies lower absolute reported scores: EmoVIT reports 83.36%, whereas EMO-R3 peaks at 77.81%. This reflects stricter evaluation—out-of-domain, small-sample, cross-matrix—rather than regression. The community is shifting from high scores to credible scores—a healthy signal that also makes cross-paper numerical comparisons riskier.

6. Open Challenges and Research Opportunities

6.1 Theory

(T1) Fixed Points and Collusion Boundaries in Self-Reflective Rewards. RER creates a self-referential system by having the policy evaluate its own outputs. Concrete question: Under what conditions is there a stable fixed point rather than convergence to degenerate text the model always labels “Yes”?An actionable formalization: Treat R_cons as a functional of π_θ and track E[R_cons] over training. Monotonic saturation at 1 without accuracy improvement would indicate collusion. Low barrier, high value: Logging training-time reward-component distributions costs almost nothing, yet the paper omits it.

(T2) Quantifying Group-Advantage Degeneration Under Discrete Rewards. GRPO's Â_i = (r_i−μ)/σ degenerates when every reward in a group is identical. Concrete question: Given binary success probability p and group size G, the proportion of degenerate groups is pG + (1−p)G; how does this proportion decrease with K reward levels?Testable prediction: From G=4→8, the baselines worsen (GRPO −0.81pp, DAPO −1.37pp) while EMO-R3 is nearly unchanged (−0.08pp), consistent with the proposed explanation that RER adds a third reward level and reduces degenerate groups. This hypothesis can be tested with a simple counting experiment and offers a mechanism entirely different from reflection.

(T3) The Information-Theoretic Cost of Structured Reasoning. SET constrains the policy's output distribution to a submanifold. Concrete question: How much entropy is lost? When is that entropy useful, as in creativity, and when is it noise, as in judgment? Table 3 offers a clue: SET alone reduces WebEmo by 0.20pp, hurting the most distant domain first. Possible experiment: Hold SET fixed, sweep the format-reward weight λ₂, and plot the trade-off between OOD performance and reasoning diversity (distinct-n).

6.2 Engineering and Applications

(E1) Make the Motivating Diagnostic a Standard Reported Metric. Specific gap: The paper motivates its approach with a reasoning–answer emotion mismatch but never reports that metric after training. Suggested experiment: Sample 1,000 rollouts at three checkpoints—before training, after GRPO, and after EMO-R3—and report think–answer agreement. If EMO-R3 greatly improves agreement but adds only 1.26pp accuracy, its contribution should be repositioned as interpretability rather than accuracy. This could change the paper's positioning at the cost of a single inference evaluation.

(E2) A Head-to-Head Test of RER Evaluator Independence. Suggested experiment: Under equal hardware and data budgets, compare (a) the training policy, (b) the frozen initial model, and (c) a stronger independent VLM. Similar results for (a)/(b) suggest limited collusion risk; if (a) greatly outperforms (b) without better reasoning quality under E1, collusion becomes a strong possibility. This is the experiment I consider most important.

(E3) External Validity Across Model Sizes. Suggested experiment: Repeat the main experiment at 3B, 7B, and 32B to test whether the EMO-R3–GRPO gap shrinks with scale. Hypothesis: SET's benefit decreases as larger models spontaneously structure reasoning, while RER's increases as self-evaluation improves. If true, the components should be deployed separately rather than bundled.

(E4) Missing Benchmarks for Online or Streaming Emotion Understanding. Existing benchmarks use static, offline, single-image classification. In companion robots, content moderation, and psychological support, emotion evolves over time. What is needed: A streaming benchmark with temporal inputs, measuring not final accuracy but the timeliness of judgment revision—when a judgment changes and whether that change is correct. The conclusion mentions sequential or interactive tasks, but these cannot be evaluated without benchmarks.

(E5) Comparable Cost–Benefit Reporting. The paper reports +25% per training step but omits total GPU-hours. Proposed standard: All RL-for-VLM work should report GPU-hours per 1pp improvement. Here, the marginal cost is 0.25 × N × 600s × 8 GPU·s for 1.26pp, but undisclosed N prevents calculation.

6.3 Emerging Directions

(N1) Make Decoupling Diagnostics an Entry Check for RLVR. Concrete proposal: Before applying RLVR in a new domain, run a Figure 1(b)-style diagnostic: remove input and answer, retain reasoning, and test answer recovery. Agreement below a threshold such as 70% suggests accuracy rewards do not constrain reasoning, making direct GRPO application inappropriate. This could become a community standard. An initial study could measure agreement across mathematics, code, law, medical diagnosis, and aesthetic scoring to map an RLVR-applicability spectrum.

(N2) Alignment Targets for Multimodal Self-Reflective Rewards. The paper's RER uses only image–text consistency. Concrete question: With audio/video, should R_cons average separate judgments for each modality or evaluate them jointly? The former may reward redundancy; the latter may obscure which modality failed. R1-Omni's acknowledged underuse of audio in §5.3 warns against the former.

(N3) Redefining Correct Answers in Subjective Tasks. AffectGPT has already encountered this barrier. Concrete question: Could RER-style self-reflection replace accuracy rewards rather than supplement them? Set λ₁ → 1 and the R_acc weight → 0 so learning relies entirely on reasoning consistency. Wasteful when ground truth exists, this may be the only route for open-ended emotion understanding without ground truth. The paper uses only λ₁=0.1 without a sweep, leaving this direction unexplored.

(N4) Ethical Boundaries of Emotion Understanding. More accurate inference of human emotions directly intersects with ethical controversies around emotion recognition in hiring, education, and law enforcement. Concrete technical question: Could SET's third step include an abstention option(“insufficient evidence to judge”), with abstention rate reported separately? Current benchmarks force a class prediction, structurally encouraging overconfidence.
