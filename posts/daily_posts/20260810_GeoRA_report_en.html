2. Motivation: Quantifiable Geometric Mismatch

2.1 The Concrete Problem

The claim is more specific than LoRA underperforming. PiSSA trains adapters on W's principal singular components, effective in SFT: its Mistral-7B GSM8K rises from LoRA's 67.7% to 72.86%, +5.16pp. In RLVR, however, those directions carry already learned general capabilities.

3. Narrative and Argument Structure

Five steps each answer the objection left by the previous one:

Step 1 · Introduce the premise RL updates are sparse ← Mukherjee et al., 2505.11711: across 7 methods including PPO/GRPO/DPO and 10 model families, RL changes only 5%–30% of parameters Step 2 · Challenge the premise's implication ★ Sparse ≠ low-rank; construct a counterexample: SVD of random sparse noise has a relatively flat spectrum like dense noise ⇒ Sparsity alone does not justify low-rank adapters Step 3 · Propose a selection rule (the real innovation) To make selected sparse regions also low-rank, selection must encode geometry, not randomness or magnitude thresholds alone ⇒ Dual masks: spectral prior (low curvature) ∪ Euclidean prior (high plasticity) Step 4 · Verify the premise Figure 6: W_Geo has a pronounced heavy-tailed spectrum resembling pretrained W True full-RL updates ΔW_FullFT also exhibit compressible heavy-tailed spectra Step 5 · Add a safety belt Frozen W_res acts as a structural anchor, termed a leash ⇒ Figure 5: KL remains low and controlled even at an aggressive 5×10⁻⁵ learning rate
Step 2 Is the Most Valuable. Rather than jumping from sparse RL updates to sparse adapters, the authors block that shortcut themselves. The method must combine geometry and sparsity, distinguishing it from simply transplanting PEFT into RL.

4. Methodology and Mechanisms

4.1 Formal Definitions

Step 1 — Geometric Priors: Dual Masks

Spectral prior (suppress high-magnitude / high-curvature components): (M_Spec)_{i,j} = 𝟙( |(Ŵ_r)_{i,j}| ≤ τ_Spec(ρ) ) Ŵ_r = SVD_r(W) Euclidean prior (plasticity of near-zero weights): (M_Euc)_{i,j} = 𝟙( |W_{i,j}| ≤ τ_Euc(ρ) ) Combined geometry-constrained matrix (union, not intersection): W_Geo = W ⊙ ( M_Spec ∪ M_Euc )

Step 2 — Geometry-Aware Decomposition

W_Geo = U_Geo · Σ_Geo · V_Geoᵀ A_Geo = Σ_Geo[:r,:r]^{1/2} · V_Geo[:,:r]ᵀ B_Geo = U_Geo[:,:r] · Σ_Geo[:r,:r]^{1/2}

Step 3 — Residual Leash

W_res = W − (α/r) · B_Geo·A_Geo (frozen) Forward pass (exact function preservation at initialization, h = Wx): h = W_res·x + (α/r)·B_Geo·A_Geo·x └ Frozen ┘ └──── Trainable ────┘ Gradient mapping (G = ∂L/∂W_eff): ∂L/∂A = (α/r)·Bᵀ·G ∂L/∂B = (α/r)·G·Aᵀ

4.2 Comparing Initialization Paradigms

C2 · Missing Competitors: Only Three Baselines Remain in Medicine and Code

Table 2 uses {Base, FullFT, LoRA}, PiSSA, MiLoRA, and SparseFT are all absent. This is substantive: the paper targets harmful PiSSA-style principal initialization in RLVR. If this is a method-level claim rather than mathematics-specific, it should be tested in medicine and code too. Conservative inference: GeoRA's superiority over SVD-based initialization currently has evidence only in mathematics.

C3 · Experimental Design: Several Advantages Are Smaller Than One Rollout Sample

AIME uses 30 questions × 8 rollouts = 240 samples, with grid step 1/240 = 0.4167 pp. Checking values:

The only genuinely robust ID evidence is OlymMATH: 12.75 versus 11.25, +1.50 pp, with GeoRA best in the column. OlymMATH is the hardest ID benchmark, a point the paper should emphasize but does not.

C4 · Metric Validity: Table 6's SH Is Nearly a Constructive Tautology

SH measures alignment with leading singular subspaces. GeoRA's construction explicitly masks high-magnitude entries, making SH ≈0.005–0.015 almost a direct consequence, not a discovery requiring experimentation. PiSSA's 0.95–0.98 likewise follows its definition. Table 6 is closer to confirming design intent than causal mechanism evidence. A causal chain would require initialization variants with continuously varying SH and monotonic OOD degradation, which is not tested. NSS is less affected because it measures spectral displacement after training.

C5 · Most Serious Gap: Compressibility of WGeo Does Not Show That Required RL Updates Lie in WGeo 's Principal Subspace

Figure 6 establishes that (1) WGeo has a compressible heavy-tailed spectrum, and (2) ΔWFullFT does too. But these separate facts do not imply that ΔWFullFT 's principal directions coincide with WGeo 's: two low-rank matrices can span orthogonal subspaces.

One number could close the gap: principal-subspace alignment between top-r(ΔWFullFT) and top-r(WGeo). The SH, ST toolchain already exists, so marginal cost is nearly zero. That number is not reported.

External Evidence Also Raises Tension: Mukherjee et al. (2505.11711) Across seven RL methods and ten model families, updates are sparse, changing only 5%–30% of parameters, but within updated matrices are nearly full-rank. If so, the premise that rank-16 adapters capture RL updates needs stronger support.

C6 · Reproducibility: No Public Code, with Sensitivity to Undisclosed Details

  • Neither the ACL Anthology nor arXiv page lists a code repository or project homepage(each checked individually here).
  • Several implementation choices are underspecified: layerwise versus global masks, per-matrix versus per-tensor ρ quantiles, randomized-SVD oversampling and power iterations, and whether Wres is recomputed during training.
  • Combined with single-run results and no SD, third-party reproduction cannot distinguish implementation differences from randomness.

C8 · Narrative–evidence tension: if ρ is highly robust, precise geometric selection may not be crucial

The paper emphasizes carefully designed geometric priors while reporting strong robustness across ρ ∈ {10%,20%,30%,40%}. Moving ρ from 10% to 40% expands union coverage from roughly 19% to 66%, a 3.5-fold range. If performance barely changes, a simpler explanation is:

what matters may be avoiding dominant singular directions, not exactly which parameters are selected. This agrees with MiLoRA: avoiding principal components alone gives SH = 0.12–0.16 and positive ΔOOD of +0.17 / +0.61. GeoRA improves OOD over MiLoRA by +1.93pp on Qwen and +0.85pp on Llama—real but modest gains.

A simple test: replace the dual mask with random retention of 35.45% of nonprincipal-component entries. Table 4 tests only w/o M_Spec and w/o M_Euc, and omits the crucial random-mask control. Without it, effective geometric priors cannot be distinguished from any strategy that avoids dominant directions.

7. Related work: the chain of inherited problems

7.1 LoRA — 2021.06, ICLR 2022

① Problem addressed. Full fine-tuning has prohibitive storage/deployment costs. LoRA constrains updates to ΔW = BA, with zero-initialized B and random-Gaussian A, reducing trainable parameters to roughly one-thousandth. Updates merge into the backbone for no extra inference latency.

② Unresolved gap. LoRA does not specify where BA should lie in weight space; A is random. If updates are restricted to an r-dimensional subspace, the choice of subspace should matter. LoRA leaves that freedom entirely to randomness.

③ The focal paper's response. GeoRA inherits the structure but rejects initialization neutrality. Interestingly, plain LoRA's ΔOOD in Table 1 is +0.86pp (Qwen)/ +0.79pp (Llama), better than PiSSA, SparseFT, and FullFT. Under RLVR, random neutrality is safer than sophistication in the wrong direction—an important implicit observation not highlighted by GeoRA.

④ Transfer of key designs.The forward structure (frozen backbone + trainable low-rank branch) with α/r scaling are fully retained; random initialization is entirely replaced.

7.2 PiSSA — 2024.04, NeurIPS 2024 Spotlight

① Problem addressed. LoRA's zero initialization weakens early gradient signals and slows convergence. PiSSA initializes adapters from W's principal singular components and freezes the residual. Reported gains are substantial: Mistral-7B GSM8K 72.86% versus LoRA 67.7% (+5.16pp); LLaMA-3-70B QPiSSA 86.05% versus QLoRA 81.73% (+4.32pp).

② Unresolved gap. PiSSA assumes implicitly that the most informative directions are those most worth training. This broadly holds in SFT but was not tested in RL that improves specific skills while retaining existing abilities. Nor does it ask whether allocating capacity to principal directions systematically conflicts with KL-constrained training.

③ The focal paper's response. GeoRA offers a direct counterproposal to PiSSA: retain SVD initialization, residual freezing, and function preservation, but change the SVD input from W to WGeo. Quantitatively, SH falls from 0.95 to 0.015 (two orders of magnitude), NSS from 0.418 to 0.096 (4.4×), and ΔOOD flips from −2.44pp to +2.10pp. PiSSA is thus both the technical source and the primary target.

④ Transfer of key designs.SVD initialization, Symmetric Σ^{1/2} allocation, Residual freezing, Randomized SVD acceleration are all inherited. The transfer is: from which part of the spectrum to whose spectrum.

7.3 MiLoRA — 2024.06, NAACL 2025

① Problem addressed. Contemporaneous with PiSSA but reaching the opposite conclusion, MiLoRA treats principal singular components as pretrained knowledge worth protecting and minor components as underoptimized capacity, initializing adapters in the bottom-r subspace.

② Unresolved gap. MiLoRA captures the right intuition—protect dominant directions—but remains purely spectral: it considers singular-value order, not parameter distribution in weight space. It does not ask whether near-zero weights (high plasticity) coincide with low-singular-energy directions (low curvature). GeoRA Table 5 says no: Jaccard is only 0.128, with 4.55% intersection. MiLoRA covers only one of two complementary dimensions.

③ The focal paper's response. GeoRA offers a synthesis and extension of MiLoRA, expanding selection from one dimension (spectral) to two (spectral ∪ Euclidean). ST rises from 0.90–0.92 to 0.96–0.98, and NSS falls from 0.125–0.132 to 0.092–0.096, about a 27% improvement. Since MiLoRA already achieves positive ΔOOD, GeoRA's incremental gains over MiLoRA (+1.93pp / +0.85pp) are the right test of whether dual masking is necessary, rather than +4.54pp over PiSSA (see C8).

④ Transfer of key designs.Avoiding the principal singular subspace is inherited and reinforced. The transfer is: one-dimensional spectral ordering → two-dimensional geometric filtering.

7.4 DeepSeekMath / GRPO — 2024.02

① Problem addressed. PPO maintains a value network as large as the policy. GRPO substitutes within-group relative advantages from multiple rollouts per prompt for the value baseline, removing the critic while retaining KL regularization.

② Unresolved gap. GRPO defines different optimization dynamics: group-relative advantages, explicit KL, and sparse binary rewards. Gradients become sparse/noisy and update magnitudes constrained by KL. Yet the PEFT ecosystem has not redesigned methods for compatibility: LoRA, PiSSA, MiLoRA, and DoRA were developed and tuned for dense-supervision SFT without KL constraints. This methodological debt directly motivates GeoRA.

③ The focal paper's response. GeoRA is among the first to address it: all experiments use GRPO (KL coefficient 0.001, 8 rollouts), and KL control becomes a central evaluation dimension. The frozen residual is designed as a leash specifically for this constraint.

④ Transfer of key designs.The GRPO objective is used unchanged; KL divergence is elevated from a training hyperparameter to an evaluation metric and design objective.

7.5 RL Finetunes Small Subnetworks in LLMs — 2025.05

① Problem addressed. Establishes an empirical finding across 7 RL methods, 10 model families: RL post-training changes only 5%–30% of parameters. Tuning only that subnetwork fully recovers test accuracy, producing models nearly identical to full fine-tuning. This sparsity is intrinsic.

② Unresolved gap.① How is the sparse subnetwork selected? The work identifies it retrospectively but provides no prospective prediction rule. ② Does sparsity imply low rank? Its measurements suggest otherwise: updated matrices are nearly full rank.

③ The focal paper's response. The response to gap ① is direct: the dual mask predicts a subnetwork in advance using only pretrained W. Its response to gap ② is indirect and incomplete(see C5).

④ Transfer of key designs.RL updates are sparse is adopted as a premise; sparse ≠ low rank is explicitly emphasized as a limitation; retrospective identification is upgraded to a prospective prediction rule.

The internal logic: shifting evaluation criteria

Most expressive subspace ──► Fastest-converging subspace ──► Lowest-disturbance subspace (LoRA: unspecified) (PiSSA: SFT view) (MiLoRA→GeoRA: RL view)

The driver is not greater technical capability but a change in objective properties. SFT learns a new distribution, where larger changes often mean faster learning. RLVR improves specific verifiable skills within a KL ball, where large changes directly mean constraint violations and capability collapse. The same initialization has opposite implications under these objectives: PiSSA gains +5.16pp on GSM8K SFT but loses −2.44pp on math-RLVR OOD.

10. Open challenges and research opportunities

10.1 Theory

O1 · Optimal subspace selection under a KL budget. Given KL budget ε, rank budget r, and pretrained W, is there a computable criterion J(S) whose maximizing r-dimensional subspace also minimizes the probability of KL violations?

GeoRA's dual mask is heuristic, without an optimality argument. This is a high-value, low-barrier gap: under a quadratic approximation of RL loss as ½ΔWᵀHΔW, with Fisher information H, the lowest-curvature r-dimensional subspace has a closed-form solution—the bottom-r eigenspace of H. GeoRA's spectral prior is a rough proxy that avoids computing the Hessian. Comparing KFAC-approximated Fisher bottom-r subspaces against dual-mask + SVD directly measures the proxy's cost using existing tools.

O2 · Compatibility boundaries of sparsity and low rank. Under what conditions must a matrix on a sparse support have a rapidly decaying singular spectrum? Give sufficient conditions and counterexample families. GeoRA empirically establishes sparse ≠ low rank without characterizing the boundary; 2505.11711 reports nearly full-rank RL updates. Random-matrix theory could address the clean question that support geometry, rather than sparsity alone, determines low-rank structure.

Compare six initializations, reporting ID/OOD and KL trajectories together. This is the direction's most important current experimental gap: the papers diagnose PiSSA's RL failure differently. Without a head-to-head, the community cannot choose between them—or rule out that they describe two sides of the same problem.

O5 · Dynamic masks.ρ remains 0.2 during training. Could evolving ρ or the mask—loose early, tight later—improve ID gains under the same KL budget? Figure 7(b)'s flat performance across ρ ∈ [10%,40%]may actually signal room for dynamic schedules: if each stage prefers a different ρ, the best static value compromises across stages, producing a flat curve.

AdaLoRA-style budgeting could adapt ρ_ℓ and r_ℓ using layerwise spectral-energy distributions or Fisher traces.

10.3 Emerging Directions

O7 · Cross-validation of geometric and entropy perspectives. Do parameter-space geometric constraints (GeoRA's Wres leash) and policy-space entropy interventions (Rethinking Entropy Interventions in RLVR) address the same failure mode? Log policy entropy during GeoRA training to see whether it naturally avoids collapse; conversely, measure NSS and SH for entropy-intervention methods. Orthogonal mechanisms may combine; treating the same problem may produce saturation. Both outcomes are informative, but neither has been tested.

O8 · Beyond verifiable rewards. Preference rewards in RLHF/DPO are denser but noisier, usually with tighter KL constraints. Does GeoRA's low-curvature-subspace hypothesis still hold? Rerun Table 6's three geometric metrics under DPO and test whether PiSSA's SH–NSS–performance relationship remains monotonic.

O9 · Mask interpretability and safety implications. MSpec ∪ MEuc What functions do the selected 35.45% of parameters serve? Do they overlap known safety-alignment subsets? Systematic avoidance would provide a mechanism for improving capabilities while preserving alignment; overlap could amplify alignment-tax risks. The experiment only requires comparing masks with existing alignment-critical parameter localizations.

  • Uses token-level reweighting based on entropy changes to stabilize RL entropy dynamics. An Outstanding Paper alongside GeoRA, it addresses the same problem through a different route—reading both is the shortest path to a broad view of 2026 RLVR-stability research. ACL 2026 Best Papers

  • Trains models to critique and improve outputs at inference, unifying test-time scaling and RL in one framework. It represents the growing direction of incorporating test-time compute into training objectives. ACL 2026 Best Papers

  • Iteratively refines value functions to guide decoding. Its test-time approach changes decoding but not weights, contrasting with GeoRA's modification of only 0.5% of weights. ACL 2026 Best Papers

Geometry-Preserving Orthonormal Initialization for Low-Rank Adaptation in RLVR (2026.06, arXiv)

  • GeoRA's most direct competitor. It diagnoses singular-value scaling → gradient-norm amplification → KL violations → collapse, and uses orthogonal initialization (B₀=0, without Σ1/2 scaling). Neither paper conducts a head-to-head—the most important experimental gap identified here. arXiv:2606.31813

Understanding and Preventing Entropy Collapse in RLVR (2026.05, ACL 2026 Findings)

  • Systematically analyzes entropy-collapse causes and prevention in RLVR. It complements GeoRA's KL-stability argument from the policy-distribution rather than parameter-space perspective. arXiv:2605.11491

LLMs Gaming Verifiers: RLVR can Lead to Reward Hacking (2026.04, ICLR 2026 LLM Reasoning Workshop)

  • Reveals verifier gaming in RLVR, warning methods such as GeoRA that rely heavily on reward curves—the reward–accuracy reversal found in §5.4 is a small instance of this phenomenon. arXiv:2604.15149

Spurious Rewards Paradox: Mechanistically Understanding How RLVR Activates Memorization Shortcuts (2026.01, arXiv)

  • Mechanistically explains why RLVR can improve scores with false rewards by activating memorization shortcuts, challenging the assumption that higher ID scores imply better reasoning. arXiv:2601.11061

Do Post-Training Algorithms Actually Differ? Scale-Dependent Ranking Inversions (2026.03, arXiv)

  • Compares 51 post-training configurations across 8 methods × 4 scales (0.5B–7B) in one framework, reporting algorithm-ranking reversals with scale. Model-size effects (~50pp) dwarf loss-function changes (~1pp), creating direct external pressure on GeoRA. ⚠️ Single-author preprint, not peer reviewed. arXiv:2603.19335

Test-Time Scaling in Reasoning LLMs: Inference Regimes, Evaluation, and Reproducibility (2026.08, arXiv)

12. Conclusion

GeoRA's contribution in one sentence: It is among the first works to take seriously that SFT's PEFT toolbox cannot simply transfer to RLVR, offering a conceptually clean remedy: change the matrix subjected to SVD.

Its strongest evidence is Table 6's SH–NSS–ΔOOD trend (PiSSA 0.95/0.418/−2.44 → MiLoRA 0.16/0.132/+0.17 → GeoRA 0.015/0.096/+2.10), alongside Table 1's structural finding that it alone improves both ID and OOD.

Three inexpensive experiments are most needed: a random-mask control to test C8's alternative hypothesis, principal-subspace alignment measurements of ΔWFullFT against WGeo to address C5, and multiple-seed reruns to make C3's one-rollout differences assessable. Until then, the necessity of this particular dual mask remains untested. What is supported is avoiding principal singular directions—a point MiLoRA already made in 2024.
