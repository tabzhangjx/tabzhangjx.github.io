Pallatom

Advancing Sequence–Structure Co-Design to Joint All-Atom Generation

PMLR 267:50786–50816 2026-10-04 Atom14 · EDM · Recycling

Atom14 coordinate diffusion/recycling raises CO-DESIGN DES-aa from MultiFlow62.74% to 85.03% and structural clusters from 134 to 291. Yet CarbonNovo leads by 20.9 pp at L=400, large step sizes induce92% all-helical bias, and wet-lab validation is absent.

Core positioning: Pallatom uses fixed-size Atom14 clouds, coordinate diffusion, and structural recycling to learn joint protein sequences/all-atom conformations without knowing side-chain element types. It improves computational co-design success/diversity at 60–120 aa, but long-chain degradation, secondary-structure bias, proxy evaluation, and absent wet-lab tests make it a viable engineering route rather than a fully validated design platform.

2. Motivation: beyond adding more atoms

Protein generators often first generate backbones, then add sequences with inverse folding such as ProteinMPNN, and verify with structure predictors. This divides joint p(sequence,all-atom coordinates) into conditional distributions—a mature pipeline with three specific gaps.

First,Side chains are not a negligible rendering layer. Active sites, hydrogen-bond networks, hydrophobic cores, and ligand contacts depend on side-chain atoms. Cα/backbone-only models can yield apparently foldable backbones without paying atom-clash or chemical-consistency costs during generation. Second,sequence and structure are strongly coupled. Backbone-first pipelines push early geometric errors into downstream repair; a backbone designable only by a narrow sequence set may still score well. Third,existing all-atom representations are costly or complex: Protpardelle’s atom73 superposes possible side chains, entangling discrete identity with continuous coordinates during reverse generation.

Main experiments sample250 candidates at each of 60,70,80,90,100,110,120 aa, totaling1,750. Strongest co-design baseline MultiFlow has CO-DESIGN DES-aa 62.74%versus Pallatom 85.03%, an absolute increase of 22.29 ppand relative increase of 35.53%. Foldseek structural clusters rise from 134 to 291, adding 157 clusters, or 117.16%; MMseq2 sequence clusters increase by 1,042 to 1,466, adding 424, or 40.69%(Table 1, PDF p.7). Joint all-atom modeling can expand designable diversity rather than necessarily sacrifice it.

This does not imply universal superiority over backbone methods. With ProteinMPNN postprocessing, Pallatom DES-bb is 89.89%below ProteinGenerator’s 93.14%, a difference of 3.25 pp(Table 1, p.7). Evidence favors the specified co-design protocol, not dominance over two-stage methods in every design setting.

3. Narrative, core assumptions, and tradeoffs

The narrative has four steps.

  1. Unified representation: each residue becomes a fixed 14×3 coordinate block. Real atoms occupy slots; missing side-chain atoms sit temporarily at Cα. Initially only backbone element types are known; side-chain slots are UNK。
  2. Geometry before identity, within one trained network: diffusion predicts denoised Atom14 geometry; SeqHead predicts20 amino-acid classes and removes virtual slots. Unlike external ProteinMPNN, sequence and geometry heads train jointly.
  3. Separate global/local tracks: residue Transformers model long-range topology; atom modules handle nearby geometry. AtomDecoder converts denoised coordinates into new pair representations fed back into the backbone.
  4. Differentiable structural proxies: atom regression, sequence CE, smooth-LDDT, residue/atom distograms, and intermediate losses train jointly; sampling uses 200 first-order Euler steps.

The core assumption is that chemically redundant fixed Atom14 slots make variable side chains batchable, with geometric-learning benefits outweighing ambiguity from UNK and virtual Cα points. Second,recycling matters more than a one-shot prediction from noisebecause identities, side chains, and neighborhoods require repeated reconciliation. Third, proxy designability predicts promising experiments. Ablations support the first two; no wet-lab evidence supports the third.

Fixed slots simplify parallelization while sacrificing explicit chemical identity. Larger η accelerates denoising/provides long-chain success but pushes toward helical attractors.200 Euler steps yield roughly 10–58s sampling without establishing a quality–cost frontier.

4. Equations and sampling

4.1 EDM coordinate diffusion

For clean all-atom coordinates y∈R^{N×14×3}, noise n~N(0,σ²I), and x=y+n, the paper uses Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion Models(EDM):

[ p_t(x)=\leftp_{\text{data}} * \mathcal N(0,\sigma(t)^2I)\right,\qquad \sigma(t)=t,\ s(t)=1. ]

Probability-flow ODE(Eq.1, PDF p.3):

[ d x=-\sigma(t)\nabla_x\log p(x;\sigma(t)),dt. ]

Weighted-MSE denoiser training(Eq.2, p.3):

[ \mathbb E_{y,n}\big[\lambda(\sigma)|D_\theta(y+n;\sigma)-y|_2^2\big], ]

Implicit score(Eq.3, p.3):

[ \nabla_x\log p(x;\sigma)=\frac{D_\theta(x;\sigma)-x}{\sigma^2}. ]

EDM preconditioning(Eq.4):

[ D_\theta(x;\sigma)=c_{\text{skip}}(\sigma)x+c_{\text{out}}(\sigma) F_\theta(c_{\text{in}}(\sigma)x,c_{\text{noise}}(\sigma)). ]

Pallatom’s k-th recycling mixes inputs/updates directly in coordinates(Eq.5, p.4):

[ r_{\text{denoised}}^{(k)}=c_{\text{skip}}(t)r_{\text{input}}+ c_{\text{out}}(t)r_{\text{updates}}^{(k)}. ]

Base objective(Eq.6) and full objective(Eq.7, p.5):

[ \mathcal L_0=\lambda(t)\mathcal L_{\text{atom}}+\alpha_0\mathcal L_{\text{seq}}, ]

[ \mathcal L=\mathcal L_0+\alpha_1\mathcal L_{\text{smooth-LDDT}}+ \alpha_2\mathcal L_{\text{res-dist}}+ \alpha_3\mathcal L_{\text{atom-dist}}+ \alpha_4\mathcal L_{\text{intermediate}}. ]

4.2 Pseudocode

Inputs: length N,T=200,η=2.25,γ=0.2,t∈[0.01,1]
Initialize Atom14 x_T~N(0,σ_T²I); all side-chain identities UNK
for i=T...1:
    1. Encode x_i with global residue/local atom tracks
    2. MainTrunk denoises once, producing coordinates/pair features
    3. AtomDecoder rebuilds distograms and feeds them back
    4. MainTrunk denoises again; SeqHead predicts identities
    5. Compute Euler updates from EDM scores;η/γ adjust steps/noise
end
Remove virtual slots by predicted residue type; output sequence/all-atom coordinates

Step3 is central: current geometry becomes relational features that alter later geometry/identity decisions. Removing recycling drops DES-aa87%→21%(Table 3), making feedback—not the Atom14 name—the performance driver.

9. Genuine contributions, reusable design, and transfer potential

Actual contribution 1: fixed slots bridge continuous diffusion and discrete chemistry. Geometry forms first, then SeqHead decides which slots exist, converting variable dimensionality into masking without directly diffusing amino-acid tokens.

Actual contribution 2: all-atom recycling is reusable feedback. Table 3’s−66 pp shows that coordinate→pair/distogram feedback matters more than one-shot prediction. Ligand complexes, RNA, and crystal cells could similarly recondition denoising on differentiable geometry summaries.

Actual contribution 3: explicit sampling transitions. Tables 2/7 exposeη as both an integrator parameter and morphological-prior control, more informative than only reporting optimal settings.

Transfer should retain local atoms/global entities/recycling without necessarily retaining Atom14. Complexes can use residue/chain/atom hierarchies; molecules explicit elements/bonds; long proteins partial latents as in La-Proteina. Functional tasks need active-site geometry, energy, or experimental labels, beyond fold self-consistency.

10. Related work: problem origins and technical lineage

10.1 RFdiffusion: structure predictors to backbone generators

Problem addressed: adapts RoseTTAFold into SE(3) structural diffusion, experimentally validating backbone generation/motif scaffolding.Remaining gap: backbone-centric outputs delegate sequences/side chains to ProteinMPNN/packing, splitting the joint distribution.Pallatom’s response: integrates side-chain slots/sequence heads into diffusion.Design transfer: retains pair/single representations and recycling, expanding backbone-frame states to Atom14 coordinates.

10.2 Protpardelle: direct all-atom predecessor

Problem addressed: atom73 superposes possible side-chain atoms for direct all-atom generation.Remaining gap: many candidates and complex identity collapse create representational/computational redundancy.Pallatom’s response: reduces to 14 slots/residue, with SeqHead determining identities/retained atoms.Design transfer: preserves coordinates carrying sequence information, moving discrete identity resolution into a learned head.

10.3 MultiFlow: discrete sequences and continuous structures

Problem addressed: jointly trains discrete-sequence CTMCs and continuous SE(3) flow matching for backbone/sequence co-design.Remaining gap: side-chain geometry is outside the joint state; DES-aa needs external all-atom interpretation.Pallatom’s response: expands structural variables to all heavy-atom coordinates.Design transfer: inherits joint nonautoregressive generation, choosing continuous geometry plus terminal classification rather than explicit discrete diffusion.

10.4 La-Proteina: addressing Pallatom scalability

Problem addressed: explicit Cα plus8-dimensional per-residue latents encode sequence/atomic details for partial-latent flow matching. Reports46 million training pairs and generation to 800 aa(arXiv2507.09466v1,§2–3).Remaining gap: sacrifices directly physical intermediate states and uses much larger data/model scale.Response to Pallatom: avoids explicit O(N×14) Atom14 costs, targeting collapse at 350/400 aa.Design transfer: keeps fixed dimensions/joint flows but replaces atomic slots with latents, marking the explicit-to-partial-latent shift.

11. Limitations acknowledged by the paper

  1. Task scope: general architectures/complex systems remain future work(Discussion, PDF p.9). Unconditional monomer results do not establish complexes, membrane proteins, or ligand systems.
  2. Scale limits: crop 128 training(Table 6,p.20) extrapolates to 400 aa, but Table 8 falls behind CarbonNovo after350, directly revealing a boundary.
  3. Validation limits: only computational designability/diversity/novelty, without expression, stability, binding, or function experiments; claims remain in silico.

12. Independent critique (evidence → implications)

  1. [Evaluation validity] Because DES-aa uses ESMFold pLDDT/aaRMSD while training relies heavily on high-pLDDT AFDB structures, generator/evaluator share predictor-ecosystem preferences;therefore 85.03% more directly means predictor-judged self-consistency than experimentally verified foldability.
  2. [Long-range generalization] Because crop 128 training yields93.3% DES-aa at 200 aa but17.9% at 400,20.9 pp below CarbonNovo,therefore the global residue track lacks truly length-robust topology priors.
  3. [Mode collapse] Becauseη3.0 at 400 aa gives92% HHH/0% EEE while smallerη yields only 4% DES-aa,therefore some gains likely come from helical shortcuts rather than free sampling acceleration.
  4. [Incomplete attribution] Because removing recycling costs 66 pp without an equal-compute deeper/wider nonrecycling control,therefore the implementation works, but iterative feedback cannot be cleanly separated from extra forward compute.
  5. [Resources/reproduction] Because data pipelines/sampling commands are available but author weights and the curated35,156-sample snapshot were not found,therefore third parties can rebuild the process but cannot precisely reproduce Table 1 or exclude database-version effects.
  6. [Statistics] Because main/ablation/hyperparameter tables lack seed variance/CIs, with SD only for timing,therefore the 22.29 pp main gap is more credible than small2–3 pp smooth-LDDT/γ differences.
  7. [Cost conventions] Because training uses about 960 A6000 GPU-hours without parameters, peak memory, emissions, or preprocessing-inclusive costs,therefore 10.2s sampling does not establish overall efficiency.
  8. [Biological function] Because results concern unconditional monomers/structural proxies without active-site, binding-energy, or functional readouts,therefore protein design here means foldable sequence–structure co-design, not a complete functional-engineering workflow.

13. Open challenges: actionable head-to-head gaps

13.1 Theory

  • Explicit coordinates versus partial latents: on identical35,156 samples, parameter counts, and 960 GPU-hours, compare Atom14 with La-Proteina-style8D latents across 100–800 aa. Report DES-aa, clashes, Ramachandran, diversity, and FLOPs/step to separate representation from data scale.
  • Recycling versus compute: compare two MainTrunk passes per step against wider nonrecycling models at equal FLOPs. Only residual superiority attributes the 66 pp gain to geometry feedback itself.
  • Sampling transitions: gridη∈[1.5,3.5],γ∈[0,0.3] with at least 5 seeds/point; characterize boundaries through secondary-structure entropy, DES-aa, and deduplicated effective samples rather than five selected settings.

13.2 Engineering and applications

  • End-to-end time/quality frontier: on one RTX4090 at equal lengths/final atom counts/effective samples, compare Pallatom, MultiFlow+ProteinMPNN+packing, Protpardelle, and La-Proteina, including postprocessing, failed resampling, and predictor evaluation.
  • Reproducible training: after frozen training lists/weights/containers are released, independently retrain on 4×A6000, preregistering±2 pp DES-aa/±10% DIV-str reproduction ranges. Missing weights currently prevent completion.
  • Blinded wet-lab tests: select100 deduplicated, length/proxy-score-matched designs from each of Pallatom, RFdiffusion+ProteinMPNN, and La-Proteina. Blind-test expression, monodispersity, thermal stability, and structure; calibrate predictor versus experimental success.

13.3 Emerging directions

  • Joint complex generation: on one PPBench split compare multichain Pallatom, APM, and PPDiff for unseen targets, interface clashes, RosettaΔΔG, AF-Multimer confidence, and experimental binder hits, avoiding incompatible internal datasets.
  • Dynamics conditioning: inject NMA-tune’s lowest-frequency mode into recycling, comparing backbone-only NMA-tune on MD mode overlap, side-chain entropy, and DES-aa to test dynamic designability.
  • Generation–understanding alignment: on shared Protpardelle/Pallatom backbones compare denoising alone with understanding-encoder alignment, jointly evaluating MotifBench, all-atom co-design, and mutation effects.

15. Conclusion

Pallatom establishes that simple Atom14 slots plus strong recycling can encode sequence–side-chain coupling without complex discrete diffusion. Its main-table +22.29 pp DES-aa with 2.17× structural clusters are substantial progress; Table 3’s −66 pp without recycling reveals the mechanism, while long-chain/helical transitions in Tables 7/8 define boundaries.

Treat Pallatom as a strong baseline/module library for explicit all-atom joint generation, not a wet-lab-validated platform. Equal-data/compute comparisons of explicit versus latent representations and blinded experiments calibrating proxies matter more than further optimizing ESMFold pass rates.
